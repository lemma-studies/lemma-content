# 1 Corinthians 11:17-34 — A Passage Study

## What This Is (and Isn't)

This is a **passage-particular study** of 1 Cor 11:17-34. It is *not* a question-driven
tldrSCAR project — the prior "Meeting Structure" project (2025, archived) already used
this passage that way and answered the meeting-structure question. This new study has
a different job: **walk the passage carefully, surface what it opens, map the positions
without forcing one answer**.

The SCAR Quadrilateral (Scripture / Christocentrism / Antiquity / Reality) still serves
as a *background-illumination* tool when a topic in the passage demands historical or
practical context. But the chapter skeleton follows the text, not the four pillars.

## Structural Proposal — Four-Movement Exposition

The passage has a natural four-movement shape that early commentators already followed:

| Movement | Verses | What Paul is doing | Topics opened |
|----------|--------|--------------------|---------------|
| **A. The problem at Corinth** | 17-22 | Diagnosis: their gatherings make things worse | Schism, "heresies must be," the Supper as actual meal, social class at the Table, shaming "them that have not" |
| **B. The tradition received** | 23-26 | The dominical institution: what Paul received and delivered | Paradosis (what "received from the Lord" means), institution words, "this is my body," anamnesis, the eschatological hinge ("till he come") |
| **C. Worthy participation** | 27-32 | Pastoral consequence: how to come without bringing judgment | "Unworthily" (ἀναξίως), self-examination (δοκιμαζέτω), discerning the body (the great crux: Christ's body? church body? both?), sickness/weakness/death as discipline, paideia vs. katakrima |
| **D. Practical ordering** | 33-34 | Apostolic regulation: wait for one another; eat at home; the rest comes later | Mutual order at Table, separation of ordinary meal from sacrament, "the rest I'll set in order" — what we don't have |

## Topic Inventory (the live ground)

This is the inventory of topics 11:17-34 opens. Each gets treatment within its movement
chapter; the heavyweights (marked ★) likely earn extended treatment with SCAR background.

**Movement A (vv. 17-22):**
- A1. Schism, faction, αἱρέσεις at the gathering
- A2. The puzzling logic of v. 19 — "heresies must be… that approved may be made manifest"
- A3. The Supper as actual meal — meal-context, agape connection
- A4. ★ Class division at the Table — the poor humiliated, the rich gorged

**Movement B (vv. 23-26):**
- B1. Paradosis — Paul's chain of reception ("I received of the Lord")
- B2. Relation to the Synoptic institution accounts (Mt 26 / Mk 14 / Lk 22)
- B3. ★ "This is my body / new testament in my blood" — survey of positions (transubstantiation, consubstantiation, sacramental union, memorial, spiritual presence)
- B4. ★ Anamnesis — cognitive recall vs. cultic re-presentation
- B5. "Till he come" — the eschatological hinge; Supper as proclamation

**Movement C (vv. 27-32):**
- C1. ★ "Unworthily" — what makes participation unworthy; is this about state or manner?
- C2. Self-examination (δοκιμαζέτω) — preparation traditions, fencing the Table
- C3. ★ "Discerning the body" — the great interpretive crux
- C4. Sickness, weakness, sleep — divine discipline as present temporal consequence
- C5. Self-judgment vs. divine chastening (paideia, not katakrima)

**Movement D (vv. 33-34):**
- D1. "Tarry one for another" — equality and mutuality at the Table
- D2. "If any man hunger, let him eat at home" — separating ordinary meal from sacrament
- D3. "The rest will I set in order when I come" — apostolic regulation; what's NOT in our text

## Scope & Treatment Decisions Still Open

1. **Greek depth.** How much Greek do we surface in the body text vs. Appendix A?
   *Default proposal:* English in body, Greek in parens for the key terms (ἀνάμνησις,
   ἀναξίως, διακρίνων τὸ σῶμα), full word studies in Appendix A.
2. **Reception history scope.** First four centuries are non-negotiable; Reformation
   debate on this passage is well-documented (esp. Luther/Zwingli, Trent). Modern
   (post-Vatican II Catholic, Believer's Church, Pentecostal renewal of Table theology)?
   *Default proposal:* patristics deep, Reformation moderate, modern light.
3. **Author voice.** The methodology defaults to "Tim Uy" pen name (Author-Pen-Name.md).
   Same here, or different? Passage commentaries often have a more personal voice.
4. **Reuse of prior project.** The 2025 Meeting Structure project has substantial source
   work (Justin, Pliny, Tertullian, Plymouth Brethren, Wesley) that's directly relevant.
   *Default proposal:* link to it from the relevant movement chapters, don't duplicate.
5. **Length target.** Question-driven SCAR targets a 6-8 page TL;DR. Passage study
   doesn't have one obvious target.
   *Default proposal:* 25-40 page total work, no separate TL;DR — the Summary at the
   end is a 3-page "what to take away," not a standalone book.
