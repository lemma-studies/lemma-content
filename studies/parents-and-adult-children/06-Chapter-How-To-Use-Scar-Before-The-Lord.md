# Chapter 6 — How to Use SCAR Before the Lord

This chapter is written for the reader who has walked through the
four components, encountered the biblical postures of §5.0, seen
the views set out in §5.3 (Views 1a, 1b, 2a, 2b, and the orthogonal
Arrangement Question), and now sits with the question the paper cannot answer
for anyone else: *What should my household do?*

The SCAR framework does not deliver its answer as a conclusion.
It delivers the reader to prayerful discernment better equipped
than before. This chapter suggests how.

## 6.1 What SCAR Is and Is Not

SCAR is a *discernment instrument.* It gathers what Scripture says,
what Christ Himself modeled and taught, what the pre-Nicene church
practiced, and what faithful Christian communities have practiced
since. It does not produce a single answer for every case because
Scripture itself does not produce a single answer for every case.

SCAR is not:

- A four-column decision matrix that spits out a verdict.
- A ranking scheme in which Scripture "beats" Christocentrism
  "beats" Antiquity "beats" Reality. The legs are ordered in role
  (as the TL;DR puts it), not ranked as competing votes.
- A way to avoid prayer.
- A substitute for the discernment of the local church, faithful
  counselors, and the household itself.

SCAR *is:*

- A way of ensuring the four sources God has actually given the
  church are all in the conversation, not just the source the
  reader's tradition emphasizes.
- A way of noticing where the four converge (great confidence),
  where they leave tension (space for faithful diversity), and
  where an individual case must be prayed through with real
  humility.
- A discipline for reading Scripture with Christ at the center,
  the early church as company, and the lived practice of faithful
  believers as testimony.

## 6.2 Walking Your Case Through SCAR

The reader with a live question — *should my adult unmarried
daughter move into her own apartment?* or *should we ask her to
remain until marriage?* or *is this suitor whom she has met on her
own to be honored, or reserved for family and church
discernment?* — can walk the case through the four components.

**Scripture.** What texts speak? Read them with your case in mind.
Which are directly relevant? (In many cases: fewer than the
tradition assumes.) Which are analogically relevant? Which of the
"difficult texts" of §1.7 press on your case in ways you may not
have wanted to hear?

**Christocentrism.** What did Christ Himself do? What did He teach?
Which of His hard sayings press on your case? Does your inclination
align with the pattern of Christ, or with a cultural template?

**Antiquity.** Did the pre-Nicene church face something like this?
What did the fathers assume, teach, or practice? Where they were
silent, what did their common practice imply? Where they spoke
diversely, what were the boundaries within which their diversity
sat?

**Reality.** Which faithful Christian communities today (or
historically) have addressed this well? Not merely which
communities agree with you — which communities have *fruit* that
attests to the Spirit's work. Which have failure modes you can see?
What can you learn from both?

Then a final component the framework itself points to but does not
name in its acronym: **the local church, the household of faith,
and the Spirit.** Present the case for prayer and counsel to the
elders you trust and to the household members most affected.
Discernment is *ecclesial* and *familial,* not merely individual —
especially in matters of household life.

## 6.3 Three Sub-Tools for the Scripture Leg

Within the Scripture walk, three sub-tools have proved useful in the
wider evangelical hermeneutical literature. They are named here so
the reader can use them explicitly.

### 6.3.1 Precept → Principle → Person of Truth

Dwight Cover (drawing on wider Reformed and evangelical
hermeneutics) proposes a three-lens reading for any passage
addressed to the household question:

- **Precept.** What does the text *command* or *forbid* explicitly?
  This is the plain sense — the "expressly set down" of Westminster
  Confession 1:6.
- **Principle.** *Why* does God care about this? What is the
  underlying good the precept is protecting or the underlying
  disorder the precept is guarding against? This is the "good and
  necessary consequence" of Westminster 1:6.
- **Person of Truth.** How does this passage point to Christ, and
  how does Christ Himself embody or fulfill the principle? Every
  precept and principle in Scripture is finally *about* someone —
  the Person who is Truth itself (John 14:6).

The three lenses answer three common misreadings. Reading only for
Precept produces legalism where Scripture is silent and license
where Scripture speaks in principle rather than command. Reading
only for Principle produces sentimentality that dissolves specific
obedience. Reading only for the Person of Truth produces piety
detached from actual practice. All three together produce
Christocentric obedience.

Worked example: 1 Corinthians 7:36-38. *Precept:* the passage
addresses a decision-maker considering whether to give a virgin in
marriage; there is a permissive verdict either way (give or keep, no
sin). *Principle:* marriage and singleness are both to be received
as callings, and the covenantal decision belongs to the responsible
party (father, on one reading; couple, on another) discerning the
good of the daughter and the honor of Christ. *Person of Truth:*
the Father Himself is the archetype of the one who gives — He gave
His Son (John 3:16), and He gives the Bride to the Son (Rev.
19:7-9). Any faithful reading of the passage must eventually stand
before the Father who gives.

### 6.3.2 The Leaky-Bucket Test

Steve Wagner's leaky-bucket metaphor (as summarized by Cover) names
a common failure of theological argument on questions Scripture
does not settle by a single silver bullet. Rather than one airtight
proof, the case is built from several partial arguments — none
individually decisive, all together substantial. A bucket with
several small holes still carries water if the water is added
faster than it leaks. A case built from several converging biblical,
Christological, historical, and pastoral arguments still carries
weight even when no single argument is a silver bullet.

Most of the household-question cases in Chapters 1-4 are
leaky-bucket cases. Almost none of the faithful views can be settled by
a single verse. All of them are held by faithful Christians on
cumulative grounds. The reader is invited to notice, when weighing
a case, whether the objection is to any single argument (which may
be a leaky pail on both sides) or to the *convergence* of arguments
across all four SCAR components (which is where discernment
actually happens).

### 6.3.3 The Assumptive-Language Audit

Del Tackett's assumptive-language audit (via Cover) asks a
diagnostic question: *what does my language on this topic assume
about the reader's culture, defaults, and practices?* If a modern
Christian reads Ruth 3 and assumes Naomi's counsel is manipulation
because "no woman today would tell her daughter-in-law to lie at a
man's feet on the threshing floor," the assumption is imported;
the text carries no such judgment. If a reader assumes 1
Corinthians 7:36-38 must be about a couple rather than a father
because "no father today would decide his adult daughter's
marriage," the assumption is imported; a first-century reader had
no such default.

The audit is applied by asking: *if I read this passage as if the
modern practice did not exist — as if I had never seen a modern
American courtship or a modern American launched adult child —
what would I most naturally conclude?* Where the naive reading and
the modern reading diverge sharply, the modern reading may be doing
more assumption-work than exegesis. The audit does not settle the
question, but it prevents the tacit smuggling of cultural defaults
into what looks like exegesis.

## 6.4 The Three Biblical Postures as a Discernment Aid

Before deciding a governance question, ask which of the three
postures of §5.0 the moment actually requires.

- Is this a **Release** moment? The adult child has spoken; the
  child is choosing; you have offered the covenantal word. The
  question is not what to do *to* them; it is whether you can
  keep loving *through* what you cannot approve. Samson's parents
  are your company.
- Is this an **Intercession** moment? The child is beyond your
  reach — ill, hardened, distant, in ruin. The question is not
  how to reassert control; it is whether you will bring them to
  Christ in prayer without ceasing. Jairus, the demoniac's father,
  and the Syrophoenician woman are your company.
- Is this a **Dedication** moment? The child is being called to
  something you would not have chosen — a mission field, a
  vocation of service, a consecrated singleness, or simply a
  costly obedience. The question is not whether you can retain
  them; it is whether you can release them *to God.* Hannah, Mary,
  and Christ at Gethsemane are your company.

Many family conflicts are misread governance questions when they
are actually posture questions. The governance question — *which
of the faithful views do we hold?* — cannot be well answered without
first locating the moment among the three postures.

## 6.5 A Worked Example — Eli and Samson

The Eli-Samson contrast worked in §5.0.1 is not only a scriptural
illustration; it is a worked SCAR example.

- **Scripture.** Judges 14:1-4 (Samson's parents overruled and
  compliant, with the providential note) and 1 Samuel 2:22-25;
  3:11-14; 4:11-22 (Eli's failure to restrain).
- **Christocentrism.** Jesus at twelve (Luke 2:41-52) — subject
  but not only to parents — is the pattern of the honoring adult
  child. Jesus in Matthew 15:1-9 — rebuking traditions that
  nullify honor — is the pattern of authority that never becomes
  self-serving.
- **Antiquity.** The pre-Nicene fathers uniformly assumed active
  parental catechesis of children (Didache, Barnabas, Polycarp)
  and simultaneous adult direct-address by the church to adult
  believers (Justin, Athenagoras) — the difference by life stage
  that Eli refused to observe.
- **Reality.** The failure of parental voice through weakness
  (Eli) is one of the persistent failure modes across all
  household patterns; the difficulty of releasing under
  covenantal objection (Samson's parents) is the persistent
  discipline of Prudential Launching and the Prodigal-shaped
  release.

The worked example does not settle any live case. It shows the
reader what walking a case through SCAR looks like when done
carefully. Every live case will demand its own similar walk.

## 6.6 Where SCAR Runs Out and Prayer Begins

Every discernment instrument has a terminus. SCAR's terminus is
the point at which the reader has faithfully gathered what
Scripture, Christ, the early church, and faithful communities
witness — and the question remains genuinely open. This is not a
failure of the framework; it is the framework doing its work.

At this point the historic Christian answer is not more analysis.
It is prayer. James wrote to Christians who did not know what to
do:

> If any of you lack wisdom, let him ask of God, that giveth to
> all men liberally, and upbraideth not; and it shall be given him.
> But let him ask in faith, nothing wavering. (Jas. 1:5-6)

And to the Philippians:

> Be careful for nothing; but in every thing by prayer and
> supplication with thanksgiving let your requests be made known
> unto God. And the peace of God, which passeth all understanding,
> shall keep your hearts and minds through Christ Jesus.
> (Phil. 4:6-7)

The framework hands the reader to prayer better equipped, not
freer of the need for it. The peace promised in Philippians 4:7
is not the peace of certainty about the right household
arrangement. It is the peace of God guarding hearts and minds
*through Christ Jesus* while the practical answer is still being
sought.

If the reader has walked SCAR faithfully and does not yet know
what to do, the reader has arrived exactly where the framework is
designed to leave a reader: in prayer, in the company of the
local church, and under the Lordship of Christ whom no framework
can substitute for.
