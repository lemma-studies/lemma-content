# Appendix A: Hebrew and Greek Word Study

## Gez: Mowing/Shearing --- *gez* (Strong's H1488)

**Hebrew:** גֵּז
**Root:** גזז (*gazaz*) --- "to shear, cut"
**Cognates:** Akkadian *gizzu* ("fleece")
**Semantic range:** A cutting, mowing, shearing --- used for both sheep-shearing and mowing of grass

**Usage in key passages:**
- Deuteronomy 18:4: "the first of the fleece (*gez*) of thy sheep, shalt thou give him" --- priestly portion
- Psalm 72:6: "He shall come down like rain upon the mown grass (*gez*): as showers that water the earth" --- messianic psalm
- Job 31:20: "the fleece (*gez*) of my sheep" --- sheep-shearing context
- Amos 7:1: *gizzey hammelek* (construct plural) --- "the king's mowings"

**Morphology:** The construct plural *gizzey* (גִּזֵּי) is a standard formation. The gemination of the zayin and the tsere plural ending follow regular Hebrew morphological patterns (cf. Joüon-Muraoka, *A Grammar of Biblical Hebrew*, §88M on nominal patterns).

**Translation comparison for Amos 7:1:**

| Translation | Rendering | Notes |
|-------------|-----------|-------|
| KJV | "the king's mowings" | Standard English rendering |
| ESV | "the king's mowings" | |
| NASB | "the king's mowing" | Singular |
| NIV | "the king's share had been harvested" | Interpretive |
| LSB | "the king's mowing" | Singular |

**Significance for the study:** If *gizzey* is the original reading, the phrase refers to a royal tax --- the king's right to the first cutting of hay for his horses and livestock (cf. 1 Kings 18:5). The locusts devour the latter growth, which is all the common people have left.

## Gog --- *Gog* (Strong's H1463)

**Hebrew:** גּוֹג
**Etymology:** Uncertain. Proposals include:
- From Akkadian *Gugu* (Gyges of Lydia, c. 680-644 BCE)
- From Sumerian *gug* (variously glossed as "cornelian," "black spot," or "darkness"); proposed by Van Hoonacker (1914) but generally considered implausible by modern scholars
- From Hebrew root *gag* (גג, "roof, top"), suggesting "the high one"
- An appellative rather than a personal name

**Usage in key passages:**
- 1 Chronicles 5:4: "The sons of Joel; Shemaiah his son, Gog his son, Shimei his son" --- a Reubenite; non-apocalyptic
- Ezekiel 38:2-3: "Son of man, set thy face against Gog, the land of Magog, the chief prince of Meshech and Tubal" --- apocalyptic enemy from the north
- Ezekiel 39:1: "prophesy against Gog" --- continuation of the Gog oracle
- Revelation 20:8: "Gog and Magog, to gather them together to battle" --- final cosmic conflict

**Historical identification:** The most common proposal is Gyges of Lydia (Akkadian *Gugu*), a powerful king from the far north/west killed by Cimmerians (c. 644 BCE). Mentioned in Ashurbanipal's annals as "Gugu, king of Luddi." Phonetically close to Hebrew *Gog*.

**Significance for the study:** If the LXX reading "Gog the king" is original, Amos (c. 760 BCE) would be the earliest biblical reference to Gog as a hostile figure, predating Ezekiel by nearly two centuries. This would reshape understanding of the Gog tradition's origins. However, the Gyges identification creates a chronological problem since Gyges postdates Amos.

## Gobay: Locusts --- *gobay* (Strong's H1462 / NAS H1373b)

**Hebrew:** גֹּבַי / גּוֹב
**Strong's note:** The base form *gob* is H1462; the specific form *gobay* as it appears in Amos 7:1 and Nahum 3:17 is cataloged as H1373b in the NAS Exhaustive Concordance.
**Semantic range:** Locust, grasshopper --- specifically a swarming type
**Related terms:** Hebrew has multiple words for locusts (*'arbeh*, *gazam*, *yeleq*, *hasil*), each designating a different species or developmental stage

**Usage in key passages:**
- Amos 7:1: "he formed locusts (*gobay*)"
- Nahum 3:17: "thy crowned are as the locusts (*gobay*)"

**Significance for the study:** The MT describes a swarm (*gobay*, collective); the LXX singularizes to "one locust-larva" (*brouchos heis*). This singularization is part of the LXX's transformation of the vision from agricultural to apocalyptic.

## Brouchos: Locust-Larva --- *brouchos* (Greek)

**Greek:** βροῦχος
**Meaning:** A wingless locust; a locust in its larval/nymph stage (before the wings develop)
**Usage in LXX:** Translates various Hebrew locust terms

**In LXX Amos 7:1:** The phrase is *brouchos heis Gog ho basileus* --- "one locust-larva, Gog the king." Key observations:
- The numeral *heis* ("one") is emphatic, singularizing the swarm
- The article *ho* before *basileus* makes this a title: "THE king"
- The personification transforms the vision from agricultural to apocalyptic

**Significance for the study:** The LXX does not simply swap "mowings" for "Gog" --- it coordinates multiple changes (singularization, personification, naming) that together create a coherent apocalyptic reading. This suggests something more than a simple scribal error.

## Leqesh: Latter Growth --- *leqesh* (Strong's H3954)

**Hebrew:** לֶקֶשׁ
**Meaning:** The late growth, second crop --- the grass or grain that springs up after the first mowing
**Usage:** Appears only in Amos 7:1 in the Hebrew Bible

**Significance for the study:** The timing is crucial to the MT reading. The *laqesh* is what remains for the common people after the king has taken the first cutting (*gez*). The locusts strike precisely when the people are most vulnerable. This agricultural specificity supports the MT reading and connects to Amos's broader concern for the oppressed poor.

## Gazaz: To Shear/Mow --- Root *gazaz* (Strong's H1494)

**Hebrew:** גזז
**Meaning:** To shear (sheep), to mow (grass), to cut off
**Related forms:** *gez* (H1488, the product of shearing/mowing), *gizzah* (fleece), *gizzey* (construct plural, "mowings of")

**Usage in key passages:**
- Genesis 31:19: "Laban went to shear (*gazaz*) his sheep"
- Genesis 38:12-13: Judah's sheep-shearing
- 1 Samuel 25:2-4: Nabal shearing in Carmel
- 2 Samuel 13:23-24: Absalom's sheep-shearing feast

**Significance for the study:** The root *gazaz* is well-attested in Hebrew and its derivative *gez*/*gizzey* is a natural, unremarkable word. This supports the MT reading as linguistically straightforward.
