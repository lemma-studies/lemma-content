# Appendix H: The "Gathering" Terminology — 1 Thessalonians 4 vs. 2 Thessalonians 2

A brief examination of whether the "gathering" in 2 Thessalonians 2:1 is the same event as the "rapture" in 1 Thessalonians 4.

---

## The Greek Terms

### 1 Thessalonians 4:17

> "Then we which are alive and remain shall be **caught up** together with them in the clouds, to **meet** the Lord in the air..."

- **Caught up** — *harpazō* (ἁρπάζω) — to seize, take by force, catch up
- **To meet** — *apantēsis* (ἀπάντησις), εἰς ἀπάντησιν, "unto a meeting" — often described as the noun used of citizens going out to greet an arriving dignitary (a claim now disputed; see Observation 3 below)

### 2 Thessalonians 2:1

> "Now we beseech you, brethren, by the coming of our Lord Jesus Christ, and by **our gathering together** unto him..."

- **Gathering together** — *episynagōgē* (ἐπισυναγωγή) — a bringing together, an assembling

---

## The Question

Are these the same event, described with different metaphors?

Or are they **two different events**?

### Arguments for Same Event

1. **Same author, same eschatological hope** — Paul wrote both letters to the same church about the same hope
2. **Similar vocabulary** — Both use language of gathering/assembling
3. **Same outcome** — Both result in believers being "with the Lord"

### Arguments for Different Events

1. **Different Greek words** — *harpazō* (catch up) vs. *episynagōgē* (gathering) are not synonyms
2. **Different contexts** — 1 Thess 4 is comfort about the dead; 2 Thess 2 is correction about the timing
3. **The "day of Christ" in 2 Thess 2:2** — Paul connects the gathering to "the day of Christ" (the day of the Lord), which has a specific meaning in his theology

---

## How the Views Use This

### Pre-Tribulation Interpretation

Pre-tribulationists argue:
- 1 Thess 4 describes the rapture (caught up to heaven)
- 2 Thess 2 describes the second coming (gathering onto earth)
- These are **two separate events** — the "gathering" in 2 Thess 2 is NOT the same as the "rapture" in 1 Thess 4

### Post-Tribulation Interpretation

Post-tribulationists argue:
- 1 Thess 4 and 2 Thess 2 describe the **same event** from different angles
- The "gathering" is the believers being gathered to Christ at His return
- Different Greek words don't mean different events — Paul uses different metaphors for the same reality

---

## Observations

1. **The only other New Testament occurrence of *episynagōgē* is Hebrews 10:25** — "not forsaking the assembling of ourselves together" — there it is the gathered community of believers, not a separate end-times event

2. **The compound prefix *epi-* in *episynagōgē*** — This could mean "gathering together" (unto Him) as a destination, not a different kind of gathering

3. **"Meet" (*apantēsis*) in 1 Thess 4:17** — Often called the classic Greek term for citizens going out to meet an arriving king and escorting him back (Peterson's 1930 thesis, adopted by Gundry, Wright, and many commentators). This is a live scholarly dispute, not a settled datum. Michael Cosby's survey of the ancient evidence concluded that *apantēsis* "was not a technical term," that "all of the main elements of Hellenistic receptions are missing from 1 Thess 4:15-17," that among patristic writers "Chrysostom stands alone in giving this explanation," and that the receptions "function more as a foil" for Paul's description — while also conceding that the text "leaves open the matter of whether or not the Christians are caught up in the air in order to escort the Lord back to earth" (Cosby, *Bulletin for Biblical Research* 4 [1994]: 20-22, 29, 31-32). The escort reading thus remains a live option (Bruce, Marshall), but as an argument rather than a lexical given. The "gathering" in 2 Thess 2 could describe the same event on either reading

---

## Conclusion

Most scholars across all views agree that both passages describe the **same eschatological hope** — Christ's return and the gathering of believers. The disagreement is not about whether they're "the same event" but about:

1. **One return or two?** — Is there one return (post-trib) or two distinct events (pre-trib: rapture THEN second coming)?
2. **When in relation to antichrist?** — Does the gathering happen before, during, or after the antichrist is revealed?

The different Greek terms (*harpazō* vs. *episynagōgē*) likely describe different metaphors for the same reality rather than different events. The linguistic question doesn't settle the timing debate.
