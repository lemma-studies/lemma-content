# Trumpet Call: When the Saints Meet Their Savior

*"For the Lord himself shall descend from heaven with a shout, with the voice of the archangel, and with the trump of God: and the dead in Christ shall rise first: Then we which are alive and remain shall be caught up together with them in the clouds, to meet the Lord in the air: and so shall we ever be with the Lord."* --- 1 Thessalonians 4:16-17 (KJV)

## The Question

When exactly will Jesus return to gather His people? More specifically, when does the "rapture" — the moment believers are caught up to meet the Lord in the air — occur in relation to the appearance of the antichrist? This question has captivated Christians for centuries and remains one of the most debated topics in eschatology.

The timing matters because it affects how we understand the nature of Christ's return, the purpose of the tribulation period, and our present responsibility as believers. But it also divides faithful Christians who hold different views with equal conviction.

This study approaches the question through the SCAR framework: Scripture (the highest authority), Christocentrism (what Jesus taught), Antiquity (how the earliest Christians understood it), and Reality (how these views are lived out today).

## What Scripture Says

The central question is **order**: Which comes first — the antichrist, or the gathering of believers?

**Jesus's sequence (Matthew 24:29-31):**
> "Immediately **after** the tribulation... they shall see the Son of man coming... And he shall send his angels... and they shall **gather together his elect**."

**Paul's sequence (2 Thessalonians 2:3):**
> "That day shall not come, except there come a **falling away first**, and **that man of sin be revealed**..."

These passages suggest antichrist first, then Christ's return/gathering. The exact interval is debated.

**1 Thessalonians 4-5** describes the rapture.

**Revelation 4-19** presents seals, trumpets, bowls, and the final battle.

## What Jesus Taught

Jesus spoke extensively about His return, but He focused more on the *attitude* of readiness than on the *timing* of events. In the Olivet Discourse (Matthew 24-25), Jesus warned about false christs, wars, famines, and tribulations — then concluded with the call to watch and be ready.

What stands out in Jesus's teaching is His emphasis on love, unity, and faithfulness regardless of when He returns. The parables of the ten virgins (Matthew 25:1-13) and the faithful servant (Matthew 24:45-51) call for ongoing readiness, not speculation about dates.

## What the Earliest Christians Practiced

The pre-Nicene church (before 325 AD) held diverse views about the end times. Some expected Christ's return soon; others focused on perseverance through trials. The term "rapture" (from the Latin *rapiemur*) appears in the Latin Vulgate translation of 1 Thessalonians 4:17, but the early Greek-speaking fathers did not treat it as a separate, distinct event occurring before the tribulation.

The earliest explicit discussions of a pre-tribulation rapture appear in the 19th century — over 1,700 years after the New Testament was written. This is not to dismiss the view, but to note that the earliest Christians understood Christ's return as a single event, with the antichrist's work occurring in its lead-up.

## The Major Views Today — The Order Question

### Pre-Tribulation Rapture

**Order: Gathering first, then antichrist.** The church is caught up before the tribulation begins. Antichrist is revealed after the rapture.

### Mid-Tribulation Rapture

**Order: Antichrist first, then gathering.** Antichrist appears at midpoint; gathering occurs after.

### Post-Tribulation Rapture

**Order: Antichrist first, then gathering.** Gathering at Christ's visible return, after tribulation ends.

## What All Views Agree On

- **Christ will return visibly and bodily**
- **The dead in Christ will be raised**
- **Believers will be glorified**
- **The antichrist will be destroyed at Christ's coming**
- **Final judgment will occur**
- **Watchfulness is commanded — be ready regardless of timing**

The one thing all agree on: watch, be ready, love one another.

## For the Reader

This study does not settle the debate — and that is intentional. The SCAR framework invites us to weigh Scripture carefully, listen to Jesus's priorities, learn from the earliest Christians, and consider how each view is lived out in faithful communities.

What we can say with confidence is this: the return of Christ is certain, our response should be readiness through faith and faithfulness, and the love and unity among believers matters more than our specific eschatological timeline.

May we "watch and be sober" (1 Thessalonians 5:6), love one another fervently (1 Peter 4:8), and look for "the mercy of our Lord Jesus Christ unto eternal life" (Jude 21).
