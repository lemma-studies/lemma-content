# Appendix B: Early Church and Patristic Sources

Collected quotations from early church writers relevant to the rapture and antichrist, arranged chronologically. All pre-Nicene quotations are from the Ante-Nicene Fathers (ANF) collection unless otherwise noted. Post-Nicene sources are included where they provide significant commentary on the rapture passages.

---

## Apostolic Era (c. 70-150 AD)

### Clement of Rome, *1 Clement* 23 (c. 96 AD)

> "Of a truth, soon and suddenly shall His will be accomplished, as the Scripture also bears witness, saying, 'Speedily will He come, and will not tarry'; and, 'The Lord shall suddenly come to His temple, even the Holy One, for whom you look.'"
> --- 1 Clement 23 (ANF, Vol. 1)

**Source:** ANF, Vol. 1
**Context:** Letter from the bishop of Rome to the Corinthian church. One of the earliest post-apostolic documents. Expects a singular, sudden return.

### Clement of Rome, *1 Clement* 24 (c. 96 AD)

> "Let us consider, beloved, how the Lord continually proves to us that there shall be a future resurrection, of which He has rendered the Lord Jesus Christ the first-fruits by raising Him from the dead."
> --- 1 Clement 24 (ANF, Vol. 1)

**Source:** ANF, Vol. 1
**Context:** The resurrection is the culmination of hope, with Christ as "first-fruits."

---

### The Didache, Chapter 16 (c. 70-100 AD)

> "Watch for your life's sake. Let not your lamps be quenched, nor your loins unloosed; but be ready, for you know not the hour in which our Lord comes."
>
> "For in the last days false prophets and corrupters shall be multiplied, and the sheep shall be turned into wolves, and love shall be turned into hate; for when lawlessness increases, they shall hate and persecute and betray one another, and then shall appear the world-deceiver as the Son of God, and shall do signs and wonders, and the earth shall be delivered into his hands, and he shall do iniquitous things which have never yet come to pass since the beginning."
>
> "Then shall the creation of men come into the fire of trial, and many shall be made to stumble and shall perish; but they that endure in their faith shall be saved from under the curse itself. And then shall appear the signs of the truth; first, the sign of an outspreading in heaven; then the sign of the sound of the trumpet; and the third, the resurrection of the dead; yet not of all, but as it is said: The Lord shall come and all His saints with Him. Then shall the world see the Lord coming upon the clouds of heaven."
> --- Didache, Chapter 16 (ANF, Vol. 7, trans. M. B. Riddle)

**Source:** ANF, Vol. 7 (trans. M. B. Riddle)
**Context:** The earliest Christian church manual. This is the complete eschatological section. The sequence is explicit: apostasy, antichrist ("world-deceiver"), trial of believers, trumpet, resurrection, then the Lord's return. Believers endure the trial.

---

### Ignatius of Antioch, *To the Trallians* 11 (c. 107 AD)

> "Flee, therefore, those evil offshoots [of Satan], which produce death-bearing fruit, whereof if any one tastes, he instantly dies. For these men are not the planting of the Father. For if they were, they would appear as branches of the cross, and their fruit would be incorruptible. By it He calls you through His passion, as being His members. The head, therefore, cannot be born by itself, without its members; God, who is [the Saviour] Himself, having promised their union."
> --- Ignatius, *To the Trallians* 11 (ANF, Vol. 1)

**Source:** ANF, Vol. 1
**Context:** Ignatius warns about false teaching on his way to martyrdom. His concern is present faithfulness, not eschatological timeline.

---

### Polycarp of Smyrna, *To the Philippians* 7 (c. 110-130 AD)

> "For whosoever does not confess that Jesus Christ has come in the flesh, is antichrist; and whosoever does not confess the testimony of the cross, is of the devil; and whosoever perverts the oracles of the Lord to his own lusts, and says that there is neither a resurrection nor a judgment, he is the first-born of Satan."
> --- Polycarp, *To the Philippians* 7 (ANF, Vol. 1)

**Source:** ANF, Vol. 1
**Context:** Polycarp, disciple of the Apostle John, uses "antichrist" language (echoing 1 John) for those who deny Christ's incarnation and the resurrection/judgment.

---

### Epistle of Barnabas, Chapters 4 and 21 (c. 70-130 AD)

> "The final stumbling-block (or source of danger) approaches, concerning which it is written, as Enoch says, 'For for this end the Lord has cut short the times and the days that His Beloved may hasten; and He will come to the inheritance.'"
> --- Epistle of Barnabas 4 (ANF, Vol. 1, trans. Roberts-Donaldson)

> "The day is at hand, in which everything shall be destroyed together with the Evil One. The Lord is at hand and His reward."
> --- Epistle of Barnabas 21 (Lightfoot, *The Apostolic Fathers*)

**Source:** Barnabas 4: ANF, Vol. 1 (Roberts-Donaldson); Barnabas 21: Lightfoot, *The Apostolic Fathers*
**Context:** Expects God to "cut short the times" (echoing Matthew 24:22). Destruction comes "together with the Evil One" --- no separation of believers from tribulation.

---

### The Shepherd of Hermas, *Vision* 4.2 (c. 100-150 AD)

> "Go, therefore, and tell the elect of the Lord His mighty deeds, and say to them that this beast is a type of the great tribulation that is coming. If then you prepare yourselves, and repent with all your heart, and turn to the Lord, it will be possible for you to escape it, if your heart be pure and spotless, and you spend the rest of the days of your life in serving the Lord blamelessly."
> --- Shepherd of Hermas, *Vision* 4.2 (ANF, Vol. 2)

**Source:** ANF, Vol. 2
**Context:** The great tribulation is presented as *coming*, with escape held out conditionally (repentance, purity, blameless service); the mode of escape is unspecified, and Vision 4.3 depicts the steadfast refined *through* the fire.

---

### Papias of Hierapolis (c. 60-130 AD)

> "The days will come, in which vines shall grow, each having ten thousand branches, and in each branch ten thousand twigs, and in each true twig ten thousand shoots, and in each one of the shoots ten thousand clusters, and on every one of the clusters ten thousand grapes... In like manner [the Lord declared] that a grain of wheat would produce ten thousand ears, and that every ear should have ten thousand grains."
> --- Papias, quoted in Irenaeus, *Against Heresies* 5.33.3 (ANF, Vol. 1)

**Source:** Quoted by Irenaeus, *Against Heresies* 5.33.3 (ANF, Vol. 1)
**Context:** Papias, a direct student of the apostles, expected a literal millennium before the final consummation. His eschatology was concrete and this-worldly.

---

## Ante-Nicene Era (c. 150-325 AD)

### Justin Martyr, *Dialogue with Trypho* 81 (c. 155 AD)

> "And further, there was a certain man with us, whose name was John, one of the apostles of Christ, who prophesied, by a revelation that was made to him, that those who believed in our Christ would dwell a thousand years in Jerusalem; and that thereafter the general, and, in short, the eternal resurrection and judgment of all men would likewise take place."
> --- Justin Martyr, *Dialogue with Trypho* 81 (ANF, Vol. 1, trans. Dods-Reith)

**Source:** ANF, Vol. 1
**Context:** Justin expects believers to be present during the millennial period and through the events leading to it. No pre-tribulation removal.

---

### Irenaeus of Lyons, *Against Heresies* 5.25.3 (c. 180 AD)

> "[H]e, being an apostate and a robber, is anxious to be adored as God; and that, although a mere slave, he wishes himself to be proclaimed as a king... setting aside idols to persuade [men] that he himself is God... so that he sits in the temple of God, showing himself as if he were God... he will endeavour in a tyrannical manner to set himself forth as God... And then shall the wicked one be revealed, whom the Lord Jesus shall slay with the spirit of His mouth, and destroy by the presence of His coming; whose coming [i.e., the wicked one's] is after the working of Satan, in all power, and signs, and portents of lies."
> --- Irenaeus, *Against Heresies* 5.25.1, 3 (ANF, Vol. 1, trans. Roberts-Rambaut)

**Source:** ANF, Vol. 1
**Context:** Irenaeus (student of Polycarp, who was student of John) provides the most detailed ante-Nicene treatment of the antichrist.

### Irenaeus of Lyons, *Against Heresies* 5.30.4 (c. 180 AD)

> "But when this Antichrist shall have devastated all things in this world, he will reign for three years and six months, and sit in the temple at Jerusalem; and then the Lord will come from heaven in the clouds, in the glory of the Father, sending this man and those who follow him into the lake of fire; but bringing in for the righteous the times of the kingdom."
> --- Irenaeus, *Against Heresies* 5.30.4 (ANF, Vol. 1, trans. Roberts-Rambaut)

**Source:** ANF, Vol. 1
**Context:** Irenaeus's sequence is explicit: the antichrist reigns and sits in the temple first; *then* the Lord comes, destroys him, and brings in the kingdom for the righteous. Believers are present through the antichrist's reign.

---

### Tertullian, *On the Resurrection of the Flesh* 25 (c. 210 AD)

> "Again in the Apocalypse of John the order of the times is laid down. This order, while beneath the altar they cry aloud for vengeance and judgement, the souls of the martyrs have learned to wait for, so that first the world may drink up its own plagues from the vials of the angels, and that harlot city may receive from the ten kings the death it deserves, and the beast Antichrist with his false prophet may bring conflict upon the church, and thus, the devil having for a season been bound in the abyss, the prerogative of the first resurrection may be set in order from the thrones."
> --- Tertullian, *On the Resurrection of the Flesh* 25 (Evans translation, SPCK 1960)

**Source:** Evans translation (SPCK, 1960)
**Context:** Tertullian lays out an explicit eschatological sequence: plagues, the harlot city's fall, then "the beast Antichrist with his false prophet may bring conflict upon the church" before the first resurrection. The church faces the antichrist.

---

### Tertullian, *Against Marcion* V.12 (c. 210 AD) --- the living saints changed under the oppressions of Antichrist

> "Both those shall be raised incorruptible, because they shall regain their body --- and that a renewed one, from which shall come their incorruptibility; and these also shall, in the crisis of the last moment, and from their instantaneous death, whilst encountering the oppressions of antichrist, undergo a change, obtaining therein not so much a divestiture of body as 'a clothing upon' with the vesture which is from heaven. So that whilst these shall put on over their (changed) body this heavenly raiment, the dead also shall for their part recover their body, over which they too have a supervesture to put on, even the incorruption of heaven."
> --- Tertullian, *Against Marcion* V.12 (ANF, Vol. 3, p. 455, trans. Peter Holmes)

**Source:** ANF, Vol. 3, p. 455 (Peter Holmes translation)
**Context:** Tertullian's own commentary on 1 Corinthians 15:51--53 explicitly places the change of living saints (the "harpazō" moment) at the crisis-point *whilst encountering the oppressions of antichrist*. The rapture is not an escape *from* the antichrist's persecution but a change happening *within* it. This is direct pre-Nicene evidence for the ordering the study argues. (Tertullian's own gloss --- *an instantaneous death* under the antichrist's oppressions followed by transformation --- is a distinctive pre-Nicene reading of the "change"; it maps to the modern ordering-question, but does not straightforwardly hand any single modern rapture position [pre-trib, mid-trib, pre-wrath, or post-trib] a proof-text beyond the ordering claim.)

---

### Tertullian, *On the Resurrection of the Flesh* 41 (c. 210 AD) --- the change owing to the oppressions of the time of Antichrist

> "[W]hich means, before we put off the garment of the flesh, we wish to be clothed with the celestial glory of immortality. Now the privilege of this favour awaits those who shall at the coming of the Lord be found in the flesh, and who shall, owing to the oppressions of the time of Antichrist, deserve by an instantaneous death, which is accomplished by a sudden change, to become qualified to join the rising saints; as he writes to the Thessalonians: 'For this we say unto you by the word of the Lord, that we which are alive and remain unto the coming of the Lord shall not prevent them which are asleep...'"
> --- Tertullian, *On the Resurrection of the Flesh* 41 (ANF, Vol. 3, p. 575, trans. Peter Holmes)

**Source:** ANF, Vol. 3 (Peter Holmes translation)
**Context:** A parallel passage --- Tertullian again commentating on 1 Thessalonians 4 --- places the sudden change of the living saints as "owing to the oppressions of the time of Antichrist." Tertullian *pairs* the rapture text with the antichrist's oppression as its immediate context. Together with the *Against Marcion* V.12 passage, this is the most explicit pre-Nicene reading of 1 Thess 4:17 as an event happening *during* the antichrist's career.

---

### Tertullian, *On the Resurrection of the Flesh* 22 (c. 210 AD) --- Luke 21:24 and "the times of the Gentiles"

> "For after He had declared that 'Jerusalem was to be trodden down of the Gentiles, until the times of the Gentiles should be fulfilled,' --- meaning, of course, those which were to be chosen of God, and gathered in with the remnant of Israel --- He then goes on to proclaim, against this world and dispensation..."
> --- Tertullian, *On the Resurrection of the Flesh* 22 (ANF, Vol. 3, trans. Peter Holmes)

**Source:** ANF, Vol. 3 (Peter Holmes translation)
**Context:** Tertullian's brief but direct patristic reading of Luke 21:24. He glosses "the Gentiles" (Latin genitive plural *nationum* — the participles in his Latin dash-clause agree with *nationum*, not with *tempora*) as "those which were to be chosen of God, and gathered in with the remnant of Israel" --- so the *times of the Gentiles* are, on Tertullian's reading, the period of *this* Gentile ingathering, not a period during which Gentile political powers rule Jerusalem. This aligns with the reading Paul makes explicit in Romans 11:25 ("until the fullness of the Gentiles has come in"), and it does not license the dispensational reading that makes Luke 21:24 a prophecy of modern Israel's restoration.

---

### Hippolytus of Rome, *On Christ and Antichrist* 14-15 (c. 200 AD)

> "As Christ springs from the tribe of Judah, so Antichrist is to spring from the tribe of Dan... That it is in reality out of the tribe of Dan, then, that that tyrant and king, that dread judge, that son of the devil, is destined to spring and arise, the prophet testifies when he says, 'Dan shall judge his people, as (he is) also one tribe in Israel.'"
> --- Hippolytus, *On Christ and Antichrist* 14-15 (ANF, Vol. 5)

**Source:** ANF, Vol. 5
**Context:** Hippolytus provides the most systematic early treatment of the antichrist. He expected the church to face the antichrist, not be removed before his appearance.

---

### Commodianus, *Instructions* 44 (c. 250 AD)

> "From heaven will descend the city in the first resurrection; this is what we may tell of such a celestial fabric... They shall come also who overcame cruel martyrdom under Antichrist, and they themselves live for the whole time, and receive blessings because they have suffered evil things; and they themselves marrying, beget for a thousand years... But from the thousand years God will destroy all those evils."
> --- Commodianus, *Instructions* 44 (ANF, Vol. 4)

**Source:** ANF, Vol. 4
**Context:** Early witness to premillennialism. Those who "overcame cruel martyrdom under Antichrist" enter the millennial kingdom --- the church passes through the antichrist's persecution before the first resurrection.

---

### Methodius of Olympus, *The Banquet of the Ten Virgins* (d. c. 311 AD)

> "Now the slumbering and sleeping of the virgins signifies the departure from life; and the midnight is the kingdom of Antichrist, during which the destroying angel passes over the houses. But the cry which was made when it was said, 'Behold the bridegroom cometh, go ye out to meet him,' is the voice which shall be heard from heaven, and the trumpet, when the saints, all their bodies being raised, shall be caught up, and shall go on the clouds to meet the Lord... as also Paul intimates, that 'the Lord Himself shall descend from heaven with a shout, with the voice of the archangel, and with the trump of God: and the dead in Christ shall rise first'... 'Then we which are alive shall be caught up together with them.'"
> --- Methodius, *The Banquet of the Ten Virgins*, Discourse VI, ch. IV (ANF Vol. 6, pp. 330-331, trans. Clark)

**Source:** ANF, Vol. 6, pp. 330-331 — verified verbatim against the ANF 6 text (archive.org `antenicenefather06robeuoft`), 2026-08-10.
**Context:** Methodius reads the parable of the ten virgins (Matt 25) together with 1 Thess 4:16-17 as one event: the midnight (the kingdom of Antichrist) falls first; the cry, the trumpet, and the catching-up to meet the Lord follow. (An earlier draft carried a *Discourse on the Resurrection* "fullness of the nations / twinkling of an eye" quote that could not be located anywhere in ANF 6; it was removed under this study's verification rule and replaced with the passage above, verified verbatim against the ANF 6 text.)

## Post-Nicene Sources (included for significant commentary)

### John Chrysostom, *Homily 8 on 1 Thessalonians* (c. 400 AD)

> "If He is about to descend, on what account shall we be caught up? For the sake of honor. For when a king drives into a city, those who are in honor go out to meet him; but the condemned await the judge within... We are carried upon the chariot of our Father. For He received Him up in the clouds, and we shall be caught up in the clouds. Do you see how great is the honor? And as He descends, we go forth to meet Him, and, what is more blessed than all, so we shall be with Him."
> --- Chrysostom, *Homilies on First Thessalonians*, Homily 8 (NPNF, Series 1, Vol. 13)

**Source:** NPNF, Series 1, Vol. 13
**Context:** The most detailed ancient commentary on 1 Thessalonians 4:17. Chrysostom uses the *apantēsis* (meeting) imagery: citizens go out to meet an arriving king and escort him *into the city*. The rapture is a royal welcome, not escape from earth.

---

### Pseudo-Ephraem, *On the Last Times, the Antichrist, and the End of the World* (c. 4th-7th century)

> "All the saints and elect of God are gathered together before the tribulation, which is to come, and are taken to the Lord, in order that they may not see at any time the confusion which overwhelms the world because of our sins."
> --- Pseudo-Ephraem, *On the Last Times, the Antichrist, and the End of the World* 2 (trans. Cameron Rhoades, from the Latin text in Caspari, *Briefe, Abhandlungen und Predigten* [Christiania (Oslo), 1890], p. 211 line 4 [sermon text pp. 208-220], as printed in Timothy J. Demy and Thomas D. Ice, "The Rapture and an Early Medieval Citation," *Bibliotheca Sacra* 152 [1995]: 306-17, at 311)

The key Latin clause (Caspari p. 211 line 4): *"Omnes enim sancti et electi Dei ante tribulationem, quae uentura est, colliguntur et ad Dominum adsumuntur"* (Demy and Ice, 311 n. 22).

**Source:** Attributed to Ephraem the Syrian (306-373 AD) but of disputed authorship; many scholars date to the 6th-7th century. Latin text in Caspari; the Rhoades English translation is printed in Demy and Ice, BSac 152 (1995): 311.
**Context:** The most frequently cited "early" text in favor of a pre-tribulation rapture. However: (1) authorship is disputed; (2) the same sermon later describes saints facing the antichrist; (3) the sense of "gathered together" is contested --- Gundry (post-trib) reads an evangelistic in-gathering of souls into the church, while Alexander, a neutral Byzantinist, "understood this disputed passage as a pretribulational transportation," calling the Gathering "an alternative to the shortening of the time intervals" (Ice, IJOBA 1 [2020]: 100-101; Alexander, quoted in Demy and Ice, 316).

---

### Augustine of Hippo, *City of God* 20.19 (c. 426 AD)

> "That which there is no doubt he said is this, that Christ will not come to judge quick and dead unless Antichrist, His adversary, first come to seduce those who are dead in soul; although their seduction is a result of God's secret judgment already passed."
> --- Augustine, *City of God* 20.19 (NPNF, Series 1, Vol. 2, trans. Dods)

**Source:** NPNF, Series 1, Vol. 2
**Context:** Augustine's most explicit statement on the ordering question: antichrist MUST come before Christ returns.

### Augustine, *City of God* 20.8

> "The devil, then, is bound and shut up in the abyss that he may not seduce the nations from which the Church is gathered, and which he formerly seduced before the Church existed."
> --- Augustine, *City of God* 20.8 (NPNF, Series 1, Vol. 2, trans. Dods)

**Source:** NPNF, Series 1, Vol. 2
**Context:** Satan is "bound" during the church age (the millennium); the gospel can spread. At the end, Satan is loosed and antichrist appears.

### Augustine, *City of God* 20.19 --- On the restrainer

> "I frankly confess I do not know what he means."
> --- Augustine, *City of God* 20.19 (NPNF, Series 1, Vol. 2, trans. Dods)

**Source:** NPNF, Series 1, Vol. 2
**Context:** Augustine's honest admission about the identity of the "restrainer" in 2 Thessalonians 2:6-7.

### Augustine, *City of God* 20.13 and 20.23

> "This last persecution by Antichrist shall last for three years and six months, as we have already said... [T]he kingdom of Antichrist shall fiercely, though for a short time, assail the Church before the last judgment of God shall introduce the eternal reign of the saints."
> --- Augustine, *City of God* 20.13; 20.23 (NPNF, Series 1, Vol. 2, trans. Dods)

**Source:** NPNF, Series 1, Vol. 2
**Context:** Augustine's sequence: antichrist persecution (3.5 years) → Christ's return → general resurrection → final judgment.

## The Restrainer (*to katechon* / *ho katechon*) in 2 Thessalonians 2:6-7

### Tertullian, *Apologeticum* 32.1 (c. 197 AD)

> "There is also another and a greater necessity for our offering prayer in behalf of the emperors, nay, for the complete stability of the empire, and for Roman interests in general. For we know that a mighty shock impending over the whole earth --- in fact, the very end of all things threatening dreadful woes --- is only retarded by the continued existence of the Roman empire. We have no desire, then, to be overtaken by these dire events; and in praying that their coming may be delayed, we are lending our aid to Rome's duration."
> --- Tertullian, *Apologeticum* 32.1 (ANF, Vol. 3, trans. Thelwall)

**Source:** ANF, Vol. 3; Latin text in Becker edition
**Context:** The earliest explicit identification of the restrainer as the Roman Empire. Christians should pray for Rome's survival because Rome's fall triggers the end.

### Tertullian, *De Resurrectione Carnis* 24 (c. 210 AD)

> "[U]nless the disruption come first (of this empire, he means) and the man of delinquency be revealed (that is, Antichrist), the son of perdition... who but the Roman state, whose disruption, being dispersed among ten kings, will bring in Antichrist?"
> --- Tertullian, *De Resurrectione Carnis* 24 (Evans translation)

**Source:** Evans translation (SPCK, 1960)
**Context:** Tertullian's most explicit statement: the restrainer is the Roman state, whose dissolution into ten kingdoms (cf. Daniel 7:24) brings the antichrist.

---

### Chrysostom, *Homily 4 on 2 Thessalonians* (c. 400 AD) --- On the Restrainer

> "Some indeed say, the grace of the Spirit, but others the Roman empire, to whom I most of all accede. Wherefore? Because if he meant to say the Spirit, he would not have spoken obscurely, but plainly, that even now the grace of the Spirit, that is the gifts, withhold him. And otherwise he ought now to have come, if he was about to come when the gifts ceased; for they have long since ceased."
> --- Chrysostom, *Homily 4 on 2 Thessalonians* (NPNF, Series 1, Vol. 13)

> "But because he said this of the Roman empire, he naturally glanced at it, and speaks covertly and darkly. For he did not wish to bring upon himself superfluous enmities, and useless dangers. For if he had said that after a little while the Roman empire would be dissolved, they would immediately have even overwhelmed him, as a pestilent person, and all the faithful, as living and warring to this end."
> --- Chrysostom, *Homily 4 on 2 Thessalonians* (NPNF, Series 1, Vol. 13)

> "Only there is one that restrains now, until he be taken out of the way, that is, when the Roman empire is taken out of the way, then he shall come. And naturally. For as long as the fear of this empire lasts, no one will willingly exalt himself, but when that is dissolved, he will attack the anarchy, and endeavor to seize upon the government both of man and of God."
> --- Chrysostom, *Homily 4 on 2 Thessalonians* (NPNF, Series 1, Vol. 13)

**Source:** NPNF, Series 1, Vol. 13 (PG 62, cols. 483-500)
**Context:** The most extensive patristic discussion of the restrainer. Chrysostom explicitly endorses the Roman-Empire reading, rejects the Spirit interpretation, and explains that Paul spoke in code to avoid persecution. The antichrist exploits the political vacuum after Rome's fall.

---

### Jerome, *Epistola* 121 (Ad Algasiam), Question 11 (406 AD)

> *"Nisi, inquit, venerit discessio primum, quod Graece dicitur apostasia, ut omnes gentes quae Romano imperio subjacent, recedant ab eis, et revelatus fuerit, id est, ostensus... homo peccati."*
>
> "Unless, he says, the falling away (*discessio*) comes first --- which in Greek is called *apostasia* --- so that all the nations which are subject to the Roman empire withdraw from them, and the man of sin be revealed, that is, made manifest."
> --- Jerome, *Epistola* 121, Q. 11 (PL 22, cols. 1023ff; Latin text verified against the PL edition; translation ours --- no complete public-domain English of Letter 121 exists)

**Source:** PL 22, cols. 1023ff (Latin verified; NPNF's Letter 121 is abridged and omits this passage; English above is the paper's own rendering of the Latin)
**Context:** Jerome interprets the "falling away" of 2 Thessalonians 2:3 not as religious apostasy but as the political disintegration of Roman imperial authority.

### Jerome, *Commentary on Daniel* 7:8 (407 AD)

> "We should therefore concur with the traditional interpretation of all the commentators of the Christian Church, that at the end of the world, when the Roman Empire is to be destroyed, there shall be ten kings who will partition the Roman world amongst themselves."
> --- Jerome, *Commentary on Daniel* 7:8 (PL 25; trans. Archer)

**Source:** PL 25, cols. 491-584; trans. Gleason L. Archer Jr. (Baker, 1958)
**Context:** By 407 AD, Jerome can claim the Roman-Empire reading is "the traditional interpretation of all the commentators" --- the closest thing to a claimed patristic consensus on the restrainer.

## Eastern Orthodox Sources

### John of Damascus, *De Fide Orthodoxa* IV.26 (c. 730 AD)

> "It should be known that the Antichrist is bound to come. Every one, therefore, who confesses not that the Son of God came in the flesh and is perfect God and became perfect man, after being God, is Antichrist... But in a peculiar and special sense he who comes at the consummation of the age is called Antichrist."
> --- John of Damascus, *De Fide Orthodoxa* IV.26 (NPNF, Series 2, Vol. 9, trans. Watson-Pullan)

> "And in the beginning of his rule, or rather tyranny, he assumes the role of sanctity. But when he becomes master, he persecutes the Church of God and displays all his wickedness."
> --- John of Damascus, *De Fide Orthodoxa* IV.26 (NPNF, Series 2, Vol. 9, trans. Watson-Pullan)

> "[H]e will come with signs and lying wonders, fictitious and not real, and he will deceive and lead away from the living God those whose mind rests on an unsound and unstable foundation, so that even the elect shall, if it be possible, be made to stumble."
> --- John of Damascus, *De Fide Orthodoxa* IV.26 (NPNF, Series 2, Vol. 9, trans. Watson-Pullan)

> "But Enoch and Elias the Thesbite shall be sent and shall turn the hearts of the fathers to the children, that is, the synagogue to our Lord Jesus Christ and the preaching of the apostles: and they will be destroyed by him. And the Lord shall come out of heaven, just as the holy apostles beheld Him going into heaven, perfect God and perfect man, with glory and power, and will destroy the man of lawlessness, the son of destruction, with the breath of His mouth."
> --- John of Damascus, *De Fide Orthodoxa* IV.26 (NPNF, Series 2, Vol. 9, trans. Watson-Pullan)

**Source:** NPNF, Series 2, Vol. 9 (PG 94)
**Context:** The most systematic Eastern treatment of the antichrist. John of Damascus summarizes centuries of Orthodox reflection. His sequence: gospel preached → antichrist arises (feigns sanctity, then persecutes) → Enoch and Elijah sent → antichrist destroys them → Christ returns and destroys the antichrist. Believers face the antichrist; no pre-tribulation removal.

### Andrew of Caesarea, *Commentary on the Apocalypse* (6th-7th century)

**On the mark of the beast (Revelation 13):**
> "He... will strive to place upon all the outline of the ruinous name of the apostate and deceiver, 'in their right hands,' in order to cut off the doing of right and good deeds, and likewise 'in their foreheads,' in order to instruct the deceived to be bold in deception and darkness."
> --- Andrew of Caesarea, *Commentary on the Apocalypse*, on Rev. 13:16-17 (PG 106; trans. Constantinou, FOTC 123)

> "The seal of the beast will be spread everywhere, in buying and selling, so that those who do not receive it will suffer a violent death from want of necessities."
> --- Andrew of Caesarea, *Commentary on the Apocalypse*, on Rev. 13:16-17 (PG 106; trans. Constantinou, FOTC 123)

**On the thousand years (Revelation 20):**
> "By the number one thousand years by no means is it reasonable to understand so many years... rather they are to mean many generations."
> --- Andrew of Caesarea, *Commentary on the Apocalypse*, on Rev. 20:1-3 (PG 106; trans. Constantinou, FOTC 123)

**Source:** PG 106; English trans. Eugenia Scarvelis Constantinou (CUA Press, 2011)
**Context:** The standard Eastern Orthodox commentary on Revelation, authoritative for the Greek, Slavic, Armenian, and Georgian churches. Andrew interprets the thousand years symbolically (like Augustine in the West) but places the antichrist's persecution before Christ's return.

## The Cerinthus Problem --- Sources on the Suppression of Premillennialism

### Gaius of Rome, quoted in Eusebius, *Church History* III.28.2 (early 3rd century)

> "But Cerinthus also, by means of revelations which he pretends were written by a great apostle, brings before us marvelous things which he falsely claims were shown him by angels; and he says that after the resurrection the kingdom of Christ will be set up on earth, and that the flesh dwelling in Jerusalem will again be subject to desires and pleasures. And being an enemy of the Scriptures of God, he asserts, with the purpose of deceiving men, that there is to be a period of a thousand years for marriage festivals."
> --- Gaius of Rome, quoted in Eusebius, *Church History* III.28.2 (NPNF, Series 2, Vol. 1, trans. McGiffert)

**Source:** NPNF, Series 2, Vol. 1
**Context:** Gaius attacks Cerinthus's carnal millennialism --- the version of premillennialism that would eventually discredit the entire view.

### Dionysius of Alexandria, quoted in Eusebius, *Church History* VII.25 (mid-3rd century)

> "For the doctrine which he taught was this: that the kingdom of Christ will be an earthly one. And as he was himself devoted to the pleasures of the body and altogether sensual in his nature, he dreamed that that kingdom would consist in those things which he desired, namely, in the delights of the belly and of sexual passion; that is to say, in eating and drinking and marrying, and in festivals and sacrifices and the slaying of victims."
> --- Dionysius of Alexandria, quoted in Eusebius, *Church History* VII.25 (NPNF, Series 2, Vol. 1, trans. McGiffert)

**Source:** NPNF, Series 2, Vol. 1
**Context:** Dionysius's description of Cerinthus's sensual millennium --- the image that Augustine would later invoke when rejecting premillennialism.

### The Bathhouse Story --- Irenaeus, quoted in Eusebius, *Church History* III.28.6

> "[Irenaeus] says, on the authority of Polycarp, that the apostle John once entered a bath to bathe; but, learning that Cerinthus was within, he sprang from the place and rushed out of the door, for he could not bear to remain under the same roof with him. And he advised those that were with him to do the same, saying, 'Let us flee, lest the bath fall; for Cerinthus, the enemy of the truth, is within.'"
> --- Eusebius, *Church History* III.28.6, recounting Irenaeus (NPNF, Series 2, Vol. 1)

**Source:** NPNF, Series 2, Vol. 1
**Context:** The story illustrates the apostolic generation's visceral rejection of Cerinthus and his teachings.

## Reformation Sources (on the ordering question)

### Martin Luther, *Smalcald Articles* II.IV (1537)

> "This teaching shows forcefully that the Pope is the very Antichrist, who has exalted himself above, and opposed himself against Christ because he will not permit Christians to be saved without his power, which, nevertheless, is nothing, and is neither ordained nor commanded by God."
> --- Luther, *Smalcald Articles*, Part II, Article IV (Book of Concord)

**Source:** *Smalcald Articles*, Part II, Article IV (Book of Concord)
**Context:** For the Reformers, the antichrist was already present (the papacy). Christ's return would destroy him. The gathering comes at Christ's return --- after antichrist is already active.

### John Calvin, *Commentary on 2 Thessalonians* 2:3

> "Paul, however, does not speak of one individual, but of a kingdom, that was to be taken possession of by Satan, that he might set up a seat of abomination in the midst of God's temple --- which we see accomplished in Popery."
> --- Calvin, *Commentary on 2 Thessalonians* 2:3 (Calvin's Commentaries, Baker reprint)

**Source:** Calvin's Commentaries (Baker reprint)
**Context:** Calvin identifies the antichrist as an institution, not a future individual. The gathering of believers occurs at Christ's single return.

### Westminster Confession of Faith (1646), Chapter 25, Section 6

> "There is no other head of the Church, but the Lord Jesus Christ; nor can the Pope of Rome, in any sense, be head thereof; but is that Antichrist, that man of sin, and son of perdition, that exalteth himself, in the Church, against Christ and all that is called God."
> --- Westminster Confession of Faith (1646), Chapter 25, Section 6

**Source:** Westminster Confession, original 1646 text
**Context:** The confessional standard of Reformed Presbyterianism formally identifies the pope as antichrist.

## Early Modern Sources (Revival of Premillennialism)

### Isaac Newton, *Observations upon the Prophecies* (1733) and theological manuscripts

> "The mystery of iniquity which consisted in these heresies and began to work in the Apostles' days, worked more and more till it brake in upon the Church and caused that Christian religion was purest in the first age of Christianity and decayed gradually till the man of sin was revealed."
> --- Newton, theological manuscripts (Newton Project, THEM00222)

> "The Christian religion was set up against the power of the sword, the Antichristian by the power of the sword."
> --- Newton, *Observations upon the Prophecies*

> "It may end later, but I see no reason for its ending sooner. This I mention not to assert when the time of the end shall be, but to put a stop to the rash conjectures of fanciful men who are frequently predicting the time of the end, and by doing so bring the sacred prophecies into discredit."
> --- Newton, Yahuda MS 7.3 (c. 1704), on his calculation that the end would not come before 2060

**Context:** Newton was a historicist premillennialist who identified the papacy as the "man of sin" and the Church of Rome as the great apostasy. He placed the antichrist before the gathering --- post-tribulational in modern terms. His warning against date-setting remains relevant.

## Catholic Magisterial Sources

### Catechism of the Catholic Church 675

> "Before Christ's second coming the Church must pass through a final trial that will shake the faith of many believers. The persecution that accompanies her pilgrimage on earth will unveil the 'mystery of iniquity' in the form of a religious deception offering men an apparent solution to their problems at the price of apostasy from the truth. The supreme religious deception is that of the Antichrist, a pseudo-messianism by which man glorifies himself in place of God and of his Messiah come in the flesh."
> --- Catechism of the Catholic Church 675 (Vatican, 1992)

**Source:** Catechism of the Catholic Church (Vatican, 1992)
**Context:** The Catholic Church officially teaches that the church passes through the antichrist's trial BEFORE Christ's return. No pre-tribulation rapture.

### Catechism of the Catholic Church 677

> "The Church will enter the glory of the kingdom only through this final Passover, when she will follow her Lord in his death and Resurrection. The kingdom will be fulfilled, then, not by a historic triumph of the Church through a progressive ascendancy, but only by God's victory over the final unleashing of evil."
> --- Catechism of the Catholic Church 677 (Vatican, 1992)

**Source:** Catechism of the Catholic Church (Vatican, 1992)
**Context:** The church's path to glory goes *through* the final trial, not around it.

## Rabbinic Sources

### Mishnah, Sotah 9:15 --- "Footsteps of the Messiah" (c. 200 AD)

> In the times of the approach of the Messiah, impudence will increase and high costs will pile up. Although the vine shall bring forth its fruit, wine will nevertheless be expensive. And the monarchy shall turn to heresy, and there will be no one to give reproof about this. The meeting place of the Sages will become a place of promiscuity, and the Galilee shall be destroyed, and the Gavlan will be desolate, and the men of the border shall go round from city to city to seek charity, but they will find no mercy. And the wisdom of scribes will putrefy, and people who fear sin will be held in disgust, and the truth will be absent. The youth will shame the face of elders, elders will stand before minors. Normal family relations will be ruined: A son will disgrace a father; a daughter will rise up against her mother, a daughter-in-law against her mother-in-law. A man’s enemies will be the members of his household. The face of the generation will be like the face of a dog; a son will no longer be ashamed before his father. And upon what is there for us to rely? Only upon our Father in heaven.
> --- Mishnah Sotah 9:15 (William Davidson edition)

**Source:** William Davidson edition (Sefaria)
**Context:** The Jewish expectation of moral collapse before the Messiah's coming parallels Jesus's teaching in Matthew 24:10-12.

### Babylonian Talmud, Sanhedrin 97a --- The Seven-Year Cycle

> The Sages taught in a baraita: With regard to the seven-year period, i.e., the Sabbatical cycle, during which the Messiah, son of David, comes: During the first year, this verse will be fulfilled: “And I will cause it to rain upon one city and cause it not to rain upon another city” (Amos 4:7). During the second year of that period, arrows of famine will be shot, indicating that there will be famine only in certain places. During the third year there will be a great famine, and men, women, children, the pious, and men of action will die, and the Torah is forgotten by those who study it. During the fourth year there will be plenty but not great plenty. During the fifth year there will be great plenty and they will eat, and drink, and rejoice, and the Torah will return to those who study it. During the sixth year, heavenly voices will be heard. During the Sabbatical Year, wars, e.g., the war of Gog and Magog, will be waged involving the Jewish people. During the year after the conclusion of the Sabbatical Year, the son of David will come.
> --- Babylonian Talmud, Sanhedrin 97a (William Davidson edition)

**Source:** William Davidson edition (Sefaria)
**Context:** A structured period of tribulation before the Messiah --- famine, heavenly voices, wars --- paralleling the Olivet Discourse.

### Babylonian Talmud, Sanhedrin 98b --- Dreading the Messiah's Coming

> "Ulla says: Let the Messiah come, but after my death, so that I will not see him, as I fear the suffering that will precede his coming. Likewise, Rabba says: Let the Messiah come, but after my death, so that I will not see him. Rav Yosef says: Let the Messiah come, and I will be privileged to sit in the shadow of his donkey’s excrement. I am willing to undergo all the pain and disgrace associated with his arrival."
> --- Babylonian Talmud, Sanhedrin 98b (William Davidson edition)

> "And so too Rabbi Yoḥanan said: Let the Messiah come, but after my death, so that I will not see him."
> --- Babylonian Talmud, Sanhedrin 98b (William Davidson edition)

**Source:** William Davidson edition (Sefaria)
**Context:** Multiple sages (Ulla, Rabba, and --- in a separate passage on the same daf --- Rabbi Yoḥanan) dreaded the suffering that would precede the Messiah. Only Rav Yosef was willing to endure anything for the sake of redemption.

### Babylonian Talmud, Pesachim 118a --- The War of Gog and Magog

> The pangs of the Messiah, as it is written: “Not to us, God, not to us, but to Your name give glory” (Psalms 115:1). And Rabbi Yoḥanan said: The verse “Not to us, God, not to us” and the entire psalm, including the verse “Why should the nations say, where now is their God?” (Psalms 115:2), is referring to the era of the enslavement of the kingdoms and the redemption of the Jewish people from their dominion. Some say that Rabbi Yoḥanan said: The verse “Not to us, God, not to us” is referring to the war of Gog and Magog, the catastrophes and wars that will befall the Jewish people in the end of days from which they will be delivered.
> --- Babylonian Talmud, Pesachim 118a (William Davidson edition)

**Source:** William Davidson edition (Sefaria)
**Context:** The Hallel psalms are interpreted as reflecting eschatological events, including the final apocalyptic war.

### Key Observations

1. **No pre-tribulation rapture doctrine** in any writer before the 19th century
2. **The Didache's sequence** is the most explicit early witness: antichrist first, trial of believers, then trumpet and return
3. **Augustine's statement** is the clearest: "Christ will not come to judge quick and dead unless Antichrist, His adversary, first come"
4. **Chrysostom's *apantēsis* interpretation** --- believers go out to meet the arriving King and escort Him back --- is the most detailed ancient commentary on 1 Thessalonians 4:17
5. **The restrainer debate** produced a dominant answer (the Roman Empire: Tertullian, Cyril, Chrysostom, Jerome) and a minority alternative (God Himself: Theodore, Theodoret), but *no* identification that produces a pre-tribulation rapture
6. **Jerome's testimony** (407 AD) that the Roman-Empire reading was "the traditional interpretation of all the commentators" is the closest thing to a claimed patristic consensus on the restrainer
7. **John of Damascus** (c. 730) summarizes the Eastern Orthodox consensus: antichrist feigns sanctity → persecutes the church → Enoch and Elijah sent → Christ returns and destroys the antichrist
8. **Cerinthus's carnal millennialism** discredited premillennialism through guilt-by-association, leading to confessional condemnations (Augsburg 1530, Second Helvetic 1566)
9. **The Reformers** shifted the antichrist's identity from future individual to present institution, but still placed the gathering *after* (at Christ's return that destroys the antichrist)
10. **Mede and Newton** revived premillennialism in the 17th-18th centuries while maintaining the antichrist-before-gathering order
11. **The Catholic Catechism** maintains Augustine's order: final trial first, then glory
12. **Orthodox teaching** (Philaret's Catechism, OCA) affirms all eschatological events occur at Christ's single return --- no separate rapture
13. **Rabbinic expectations** of tribulation before the Messiah provide deep Jewish background for the NT texts
14. **Watchfulness** is the consistent call across all traditions
