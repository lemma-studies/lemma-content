# Appendix A: Greek Word Study

This appendix collects the key Greek terms relevant to the rapture/antichrist timing question. (For the Aramaic terminology of Daniel 7, see Appendix G.)

---

## ἁρπάζω (harpazō) — To Catch Up, Seize

**Greek:** ἁρπάζω (harpazō)
**Strong's:** G726
**Transliteration:** harpazō

### Root Meaning

From ἅρπαξ (harpax) — rapacious, seizing. The root relates to violent, forceful seizure. The verb means to seize, catch away, take by force, pluck.

### Semantic Range

- Seize with force or violence (Matthew 12:29 — binding the strong man to spoil his goods)
- Snatch away (Matthew 13:19 — "the wicked one catcheth away" that which was sown)
- Take or obtain (John 6:15 — "would take him by force to make him a king")
- Catch up, rapture (Acts 8:39 — "the Spirit caught away Philip")
- Catch up to (2 Corinthians 12:2, 4 — "caught up to the third heaven")

### Usage in Key Passages

**1 Thessalonians 4:17:**
> "Then we which are alive and remain shall be **caught up** together with them in the clouds, to meet the Lord in the air"

- "Caught up" is *harpazō* — ἁρπαγησόμεθα, future passive indicative
- The passive voice suggests God/Christ is the agent doing the catching up
- The verb itself denotes sudden, forcible seizure — a snatching, not a gradual ascent

**Acts 8:39:**
> "And when they were come up out of the water, the Spirit of the Lord caught away Philip"

- Philip was supernaturally transported
- Same verb, *harpazō* (here aorist active: ἥρπασεν, "the Spirit... caught away Philip")
- Shows the word can describe physical transportation

**2 Corinthians 12:2, 4:**
> "That man was **caught up** to the third heaven... **caught up** into paradise"

- Paul describes being caught up to heaven
- Same word *harpazō*
- This supports the "removal" sense

**Matthew 13:19:**
> "Then cometh the wicked one, and catcheth away that which was sown in his heart"

- The devil actively steals the word
- Different sense (stealing rather than rapture)

### Translation Comparison

| Translation | 1 Thess 4:17 | Notes |
|-------------|--------------|-------|
| KJV | "caught up" | Primary translation |
| ESV | "caught up" | Same |
| NASB | "caught up" | Same |
| LSB | "caught up" | Same |
| NIV | "caught up" | Same |
| LXX | N/A (NT passage) | — |

### Significance for the Study

The term clearly indicates a forceful, sudden removal. Whether it describes:
- Removal TO heaven (going up to be with Christ)
- Gathering TO meet Christ in the air (Christ comes down, we go up to meet Him)

The preposition *syn* (together with) in "caught up together with them" suggests a gathering — believers are caught up TOGETHER with the resurrected dead.

## ἀντίχριστος (antichristos) — Antichrist

**Greek:** ἀντίχριστος (antichristos)
**Strong's:** G500
**Transliteration:** antichristos

### Root Meaning

From ἀντί (anti) — against, instead of + Χριστός (Christos) — Christ, anointed one.

The term means:
- Against Christ (opposing Christ)
- Instead of Christ (usurping Christ's role)
- Anti-Christ (anti = complete opposition)

### Semantic Range

- A specific end-time figure (1 John 2:18)
- False christs generally (Matthew 24:5, 24)
- The spirit of antichrist (1 John 4:3)
- Those who deny Christ (1 John 2:22)

### Usage in Key Passages

**1 John 2:18:**
> "Little children, it is the last time: and as ye have heard that **antichrist** shall come, even now are there many **antichrists**; whereby we know that it is the last time"

- Antichrist is both singular (shall come) and plural (many antichrists)
- The singular may be a representative individual
- The plural includes all who deny Christ

**1 John 2:22:**
> "Who is a liar but he that denieth that Jesus is the Christ? The same is **antichrist**, that denieth the Father and the Son"

- Antichrist = one who denies Jesus is the Christ
- Also = one who denies the Father and the Son
- Denial of both the Son and the Father (Christological and Patrological)

**2 John 1:7:**
> "For many deceivers are entered into the world, who confess not that Jesus Christ is come in the flesh. This is a deceiver and an **antichrist**"

- The false teaching: Christ did not come in flesh
- This is "the spirit of antichrist" (1 John 4:3)

### Translation Comparison

| Translation | 1 John 2:18 | Notes |
|-------------|-------------|-------|
| KJV | "antichrist" | Primary |
| ESV | "antichrist" | Same |
| NASB | "antichrist" | Same |
| LSB | "antichrist" | Same |
| NIV | "antichrist" | Same |

### Significance for the Study

The term appears only in 1 and 2 John in the New Testament. While the singular "antichrist" points to an end-time figure, the plural "antichrists" shows the term also applies to current false teachers.

The 2 Thessalonians 2 "man of sin" is conceptually related but uses different terminology. Whether these refer to the same individual is debated.

## παρουσία (parousia) — Coming, Presence

**Greek:** παρουσία (parousia)
**Strong's:** G3952
**Transliteration:** parousia

### Root Meaning

From πάρειμι (pareimi) — to be present, to have come. The term means presence, arrival, coming — used of both persons and things.

### Semantic Range

- Physical presence (2 Corinthians 10:10 — "his bodily presence")
- Arrival/coming of a person (1 Corinthians 16:17 — "the coming of Stephanas")
- Eschatological return of Christ (1 Thessalonians 2:19 — "at our Lord Jesus Christ's coming")

### Usage in Key Passages

**1 Thessalonians 2:19:**
> "For what is our hope, or joy, or crown of rejoicing? Are not even ye in the presence of our Lord Jesus Christ at his coming (*parousia*)?"

- "Coming" is *parousia*
- Used of Christ's return

**1 Thessalonians 3:13:**
> "To the end he may stablish your hearts unblameable in holiness before God, even our Father, at the coming (*parousia*) of our Lord Jesus Christ with all his saints"

- Christ's return with the saints
- Again, *parousia*

**1 Thessalonians 4:15:**
> "For this we say unto you by the word of the Lord, that we which are alive and remain unto the coming (*parousia*) of the Lord"

- The same word
- Paul's teaching about the return

**1 Thessalonians 5:23:**
> "And the very God of peace sanctify you wholly; and I pray God your whole spirit and soul and body be preserved blameless unto the coming (*parousia*) of our Lord Jesus Christ"

- Same usage
- Preservation *until* the coming

### Translation Comparison

| Translation | 1 Thess 4:15 | Notes |
|-------------|--------------|-------|
| KJV | "coming" | Primary |
| ESV | "coming" | Same |
| NASB | "coming" | Same |
| LSB | "coming" | Same |
| NIV | "coming" | Same |

### Significance for the Study

The term *parousia* consistently refers to Christ's return. Whether this "coming" includes both the rapture (meeting in the air) and the return to earth (establishing the kingdom) is the key question.

Pre-tribulationists argue for two *parousiai* (plural): one for the church (rapture), one for the world (revelation). Post-tribulationists argue for one *parousia* with multiple aspects.

## σάλπιγξ (salpinx) — Trumpet

**Greek:** σάλπιγξ (salpinx)
**Strong's:** G4536
**Transliteration:** salpinx

### Root Meaning

A trumpet — a brass instrument used in military and religious contexts for signaling, gathering, alarms, and announcements.

### Semantic Range

- Military trumpet (1 Corinthians 14:8)
- Eschatological trumpet (1 Corinthians 15:52; 1 Thessalonians 4:16)
- Angelic trumpet (Revelation)

### Usage in Key Passages

**1 Corinthians 15:52:**
> "In a moment, in the twinkling of an eye, at the last trump: for the trumpet shall sound, and the dead shall be raised incorruptible, and we shall be changed"

- "Last trump" = *eschatē salpinx* (ἐσχάτη σάλπιγξ)
- The trumpet signals the resurrection
- Used with rapture/transformation

**1 Thessalonians 4:16:**
> "For the Lord himself shall descend from heaven with a shout, with the voice of the archangel, and with the trump of God"

- "Trump of God" = *salpinx theou*
- Divine trumpet, not human
- Accompanies Christ's descent

### Significance for the Study

The trumpet is a gathering signal — it calls people together. In eschatological contexts, it signals:
- The assembly/convocation of God's people
- The announcement of Christ's arrival
- The beginning of the final judgment

The trumpet in 1 Thessalonians 4:16 accompanies Christ's descent (not our ascent). This is part of what makes the timing debate complex — is the trumpet signaling our catching up, or is it signaling Christ coming to earth?

Note also that identifying eschatological trumpets *across* books is itself contested territory: the mid-trib view equates 1 Corinthians 15:52 with Revelation 11:15, the pre-wrath view with Joel 2:1, and critics on both flanks reject the equations (Stearns, BSac 99 [1942]: 124-26; Svigel, TrinJ 22 [2001]: 41-42; Cole, DBSJ 9 [2004]: 40 — "The two trumpets are not synonymous").

## ἀποστασία (apostasia) --- Falling Away, Apostasy

**Greek:** ἀποστασία (apostasia)
**Strong's:** G646
**Transliteration:** apostasia

### Root Meaning

From ἀφίστημι (aphistēmi) --- to stand away from, to depart. The noun means departure, defection, apostasy, revolt.

### Semantic Range

- Political revolt or defection (used in classical Greek for military rebellion)
- Religious apostasy, abandonment of faith (2 Thessalonians 2:3)
- Departure, withdrawal (some early English translations rendered "departing")

### Usage in Key Passages

**2 Thessalonians 2:3:**
> "Let no man deceive you by any means: for that day shall not come, except there come a **falling away** first, and that man of sin be revealed"

- "Falling away" is *apostasia*
- This is the great apostasy that must precede the Day of the Lord
- The KJV, ESV, NASB all render "falling away" or "apostasy"

**Acts 21:21:**
> "And they are informed of thee, that thou teachest all the Jews which are among the Gentiles to forsake [*apostasian*] Moses"

- The only other NT occurrence
- Here it means departure from the Law of Moses

### Translation Comparison

| Translation | 2 Thess 2:3 | Notes |
|-------------|-------------|-------|
| KJV | "a falling away" | Traditional rendering |
| ESV | "the rebellion" | Emphasizes revolt |
| NASB | "the apostasy" | Transliterates the Greek |
| LSB | "the apostasy" | Same |
| NIV | "the rebellion" | Same as ESV |

### Significance for the Study

A minority view holds that *apostasia* means "departure" (physical departure = rapture), making 2 Thessalonians 2:3 itself a rapture reference. The view can be traced to E. Schuyler English's series "Re-Thinking the Rapture" (*Our Hope*, October 1949-March 1950; per Combs, DBSJ 3 [1998]: 63) and has been defended more recently by H. Wayne House and John Sweigart (CTJ 5 [2001]: 186-204). However, the overwhelming consensus among Greek lexicons (BDAG, LSJ, Thayer) and early translations is that the word means religious defection --- and pre-tribulational scholars themselves largely reject the rapture reading: Combs (himself pre-trib) concludes that *apostasia* "should not be used as evidence for the pretribulational Rapture" (DBSJ 3 [1998]: 87), and Dean notes the view "is widely rejected" (BSac 168 [2011]: 206). The Tyndale Bible (1526) rendered it "a departynge," but this likely meant departure from the faith, not physical rapture --- Combs finds the appeal to pre-KJV versions has "no merit whatsoever" as evidence of spatial departure, noting that the original Wycliffe edition apparently rendered the word "discencioun" (dissension), not "departynge" (DBSJ 3 [1998]: 77, 86).

## ὀργή (orgē) --- Wrath

**Greek:** ὀργή (orgē)
**Strong's:** G3709
**Transliteration:** orgē

### Root Meaning

Settled anger, indignation, wrath. Distinguished from *thymos* (θυμός, G2372), which is passionate, explosive anger. *Orgē* is deliberate, settled divine judgment.

### Usage in Key Passages

**1 Thessalonians 1:10:**
> "And to wait for his Son from heaven... even Jesus, which delivered us from the **wrath** to come"

**1 Thessalonians 5:9:**
> "For God hath not appointed us to **wrath**, but to obtain salvation"

**Revelation 6:16-17:**
> "Hide us from the face of him that sitteth on the throne, and from the **wrath** of the Lamb: For the great day of his **wrath** is come"

**Romans 5:9:**
> "Much more then, being now justified by his blood, we shall be saved from **wrath** through him"

### Significance for the Study

The meaning of *orgē* is central to the timing debate:
- **Pre-tribulationists** argue that believers are delivered from the entire tribulation period, which they identify as God's wrath
- **Post-tribulationists** distinguish between God's *orgē* (final eschatological judgment, which believers escape) and tribulation/persecution (which believers endure). They note that *thlipsis* (tribulation) and *orgē* (wrath) are different concepts in Paul's letters

## θλῖψις (thlipsis) --- Tribulation, Affliction

**Greek:** θλῖψις (thlipsis)
**Strong's:** G2347
**Transliteration:** thlipsis

### Root Meaning

From θλίβω (thlibō) --- to press, to squeeze. The noun means pressure, affliction, tribulation, distress.

### Semantic Range

- General affliction in this world (John 16:33 --- "In the world ye shall have tribulation")
- Persecution for the faith (Acts 14:22 --- "through much tribulation enter into the kingdom")
- The eschatological "great tribulation" (Matthew 24:21 --- *thlipsis megalē*)

### Usage in Key Passages

**John 16:33:**
> "In the world ye shall have **tribulation**: but be of good cheer; I have overcome the world"

**Matthew 24:21:**
> "For then shall be **great tribulation**, such as was not since the beginning of the world"

**Revelation 7:14:**
> "These are they which came out of **great tribulation**, and have washed their robes, and made them white in the blood of the Lamb"

**Acts 14:22:**
> "Confirming the souls of the disciples, and exhorting them to continue in the faith, and that we must through much **tribulation** enter into the kingdom of God"

### Significance for the Study

*Thlipsis* is NOT the same word as *orgē* (wrath). This distinction is critical:
- Jesus promises His followers *will* have *thlipsis* (tribulation/affliction) --- John 16:33
- Paul says believers are NOT appointed to *orgē* (wrath) --- 1 Thessalonians 5:9
- The question is whether the "great tribulation" of Matthew 24:21 is *thlipsis* (which believers endure) or *orgē* (from which believers are delivered)

## ἀπάντησις (apantēsis) --- Meeting

**Greek:** ἀπάντησις (apantēsis)
**Strong's:** G529
**Transliteration:** apantēsis

### Root Meaning

A meeting, encounter --- specifically, the formal reception of a visiting dignitary. In the Greco-Roman world, when an emperor or general approached a city, citizens would go out to *meet* (*apantēsis*) the dignitary and then escort him back into the city.

### Usage in Key Passages

**1 Thessalonians 4:17:**
> "Then we which are alive and remain shall be caught up together with them in the clouds, to **meet** the Lord in the air"

**Matthew 25:6:**
> "And at midnight there was a cry made, Behold, the bridegroom cometh; go ye out to **meet** him"

**Acts 28:15:**
> "And from thence, when the brethren heard of us, they came to **meet** us as far as Appii Forum"

### Significance for the Study

This word carries enormous weight in the timing debate. In all other NT and contemporary uses, *apantēsis* describes going out to meet someone who is *coming toward you* and then escorting them back to where you came from. The citizens of Rome went out to meet Paul and escorted him *into* Rome --- they did not leave Rome permanently.

- **Post-tribulationists** argue this proves the rapture is believers going up to meet Christ and then escorting Him *back to earth* --- not going to heaven
- **Pre-tribulationists** argue that the "air" (*aēr*) is the destination, not just a meeting point

This technical term is one of the strongest lexical arguments for a single-event return.
