# Chapter 6: Synthesis and Proposed Solution

## Overview

This chapter brings together all four components of the SCAR Quadrilateral (Scripture, Christocentrism, Antiquity, Reality) to synthesize findings and propose practical solutions for structuring meetings that honor both the Lord's Supper and enable mutual edification.

**Key Principle:** The ultimate test is not perfect structure, but whether the structure helps us **love one another as Jesus loved us** and be **one as Jesus prayed for us to be**.

---

## PART 1: SYNTHESIS OF ALL FOUR COMPONENTS

### 1. Scripture Component - Key Findings

**What Scripture Says:**
- **Lord's Supper is commanded** - "Do this in remembrance of me" (Luke 22:19)
- **Remembrance is primary** - Focus on Christ's death
- **Mutual care is required** - "Wait for one another" (1 Corinthians 11:33)
- **Unity is essential** - No divisions (1 Corinthians 1:10, 11:17-18)
- **Participation is encouraged** - "Each one has" (1 Corinthians 14:26)
- **Order is required** - "Decently and in order" (1 Corinthians 14:40)
- **Building up is the purpose** - "For building up" (1 Corinthians 14:26)
- **Love is the foundation** - Without love, nothing matters (1 Corinthians 13)

**The Ambiguity:**
- Scripture doesn't explicitly say whether Lord's Supper and participatory worship must be integrated or separated
- 1 Corinthians 11 and 14 could be same meeting or different meetings
- Both patterns can be supported from Scripture

**Conclusion:** Scripture is clear on **principles** (love, unity, remembrance, mutual edification, order) but **ambiguous on exact structure**.

---

### 2. Christocentrism Component - Key Findings

**What Jesus Taught:**
- **Love one another** - "As I have loved you" (John 13:34-35) - **MOST IMPORTANT**
- **Unity** - "That they may all be one" (John 17:20-23) - **ESSENTIAL**
- **Mutual service** - "I have given you an example" (John 13:12-17)
- **Remembrance** - "Do this in remembrance of me" (Luke 22:19) - **PRIMARY PURPOSE**
- **Mutual edification** - "Where two or three are gathered in my name" (Matthew 18:20)

**What the Last Supper Actually Shows:**
- Jesus teaching (primary teacher)
- Some questions from disciples
- Not fully participatory "each one has" pattern
- Focus on remembrance and teaching

**What the Bible Actually Shows:**
- **Acts 20:7** - Paul talked until midnight (one person, extended teaching)
- **Agabus** - Participatory prophecy (Acts 11:28, 21:10)
- **Most examples** - One person teaching for extended time
- **Some participation** - Prophecy, questions, but primary pattern is teaching

**Early Church Fathers on Jesus's Teachings:**
- **Clement of Rome** - Love is essential, prevents division
- **Ignatius of Antioch** - Unity is essential, "one prayer, one mind"
- **Didache** - Mutual service, reverent remembrance

**Conclusion:** Jesus emphasized **love and unity as most important**, more than structure. Teaching is valid (can be extended), but love and unity are primary.

---

### 3. Antiquity Component - Key Findings

**What Early Church Did:**
- **Both integrated and separated models existed**
  - Integrated: Justin Martyr (c. 150 AD) - Reading, teaching, prayer, Eucharist all together
  - Separated: Pliny (c. 112 AD) - Two meetings; Tertullian (c. 197 AD) - Eucharist morning, agape evening
- **Participatory elements were present** - Responsive prayer, individual singing, prophetic contributions
- **Reverence was maintained** - Preparation, confession, reconciliation required
- **Love and unity were priorities** - Reconciliation before communion, care for absentees
- **Structure increased over time** - Early more flexible, later more structured, but participatory elements remained

**Historical Development:**
- **First century:** Appears integrated (Acts 2, Acts 20)
- **Early second century (c. 112):** Two meetings (Pliny)
- **Mid-second century (c. 150):** Integrated Sunday meeting (Justin)
- **Late second century (c. 197):** Separated - Eucharist morning, agape evening (Tertullian)
- **Early third century (c. 215):** Clearly separated (Hippolytus)

**Conclusion:** Early church shows **flexibility** - both integration and separation are historically valid. **Principles matter more than structure** - love, unity, reverence, participation.

---

### 4. Reality Component - Key Findings

**What Modern Groups Do:**
- **Plymouth Brethren:** Lord's Supper as primary purpose, participatory but focused on Supper
- **SFMI:** Open meetings with clear guidelines, balance of freedom and order
- **Shield of Faith Fellowship:** Participatory worship, "body functioning" model, 1000+ churches
- **NTRF:** Explicit 1 Corinthians 14:26 advocacy
- **House churches:** Various structures, some integrated, some separated

**Patterns in Modern Practice:**
- **Both integration and separation work** - Depends on context
- **Participatory elements are valued** - All successful groups have them
- **Order and boundaries are needed** - Structure prevents chaos
- **Love and unity guide decisions** - More important than being "right"
- **Practical wisdom is needed** - Time limits, focus, discernment

**Current Brethren Tension:**
- Need for focused, reverent remembrance
- Need for open participatory teaching
- Some want separate time for doctrinal teaching
- How to honor both without diluting either?

**Conclusion:** Modern examples show that **both integration and separation can work**, as long as they serve love, unity, reverence, and mutual edification. **Structure is secondary to these principles**.

## PART 3: REMAINING TENSIONS

### 1. Integration vs. Separation

**Tension:** Should Lord's Supper and participatory worship be in the same meeting or separate?

**Scripture:** Ambiguous - could support either

**Christocentrism:** Remembrance is primary, teaching is important - both needed

**Antiquity:** Both patterns existed

**Reality:** Both patterns work

**Resolution:** **Either can work** - Choose what serves love, unity, and mutual edification best in your context.

---

### 2. Focus vs. Openness

**Tension:** How to balance focused reverence (Lord's Supper) with open participation (teaching, doctrine)?

**Scripture:** Both are important

**Christocentrism:** Remembrance is primary, but teaching is important

**Antiquity:** Participatory elements present even in reverent contexts

**Reality:** Successful groups have both - reverence and participation

**Resolution:** **Both are possible** - Can have reverent Lord's Supper with some participation, or separate times for each.

---

### 3. Reverence vs. Participation

**Tension:** Are reverence and participation opposed?

**Scripture:** Both are required

**Christocentrism:** Reverence is essential, some participation is possible

**Antiquity:** Reverence and participation went together

**Reality:** Successful groups have both

**Resolution:** **They go together** - Not opposed, but complementary. Can have reverent participation.

---

### 4. Teaching Length vs. Participation

**Tension:** Extended teaching (Paul until midnight) vs. "each one has" participation?

**Scripture:** Both patterns shown - Paul until midnight, but also "each one has"

**Christocentrism:** Teaching is valid (can be extended), but some participation is biblical

**Antiquity:** Both patterns existed

**Reality:** Both patterns work

**Resolution:** **Both are valid** - Can have extended teaching, or participatory "each one has," or both in different parts of meeting.

## PART 4: PROPOSED SOLUTIONS

### Solution 1: Two-Part Meeting Model

**Structure:**
- **Part 1: Focused Lord's Supper** (30-45 minutes)
  - Reverent, Christ-centered
  - Focus on remembrance
  - Participatory but focused (hymns, prayers, Scripture related to Christ's death)
  - Breaking bread and sharing cup
  - Clear boundaries: Focus on Lord's Supper, not general doctrine

- **Transition:** Brief pause, prayer, or song

- **Part 2: Open Participatory Worship** (45-60 minutes)
  - Open for teaching, doctrine, spiritual gifts
  - "Each one has" pattern
  - Mutual edification
  - Doctrinal teaching welcome
  - Prophecy, teaching, testimonies, questions
  - Clear guidelines (time limits, focus on building up)

**Advantages:**
- Honors both reverence and participation
- Clear boundaries prevent dilution
- Allows doctrinal teaching in appropriate time
- Maintains focus on Lord's Supper
- Enables mutual edification

**Challenges:**
- Requires clear transition
- May feel like "two meetings"
- Need to maintain unity between parts

**Biblical Support:**
- Acts 20:7 - Breaking bread meeting included teaching (could be seen as two parts)
- 1 Corinthians 11 & 14 - Could be two parts of same meeting
- Early church - Both patterns existed

**Early Church Precedent:**
- Pliny - Two meetings (morning worship, later meal)
- Could be adapted to two-part meeting

**Modern Examples:**
- Some Brethren considering "two meetings"
- SFMI has guidelines for different parts of meeting

---

### Solution 2: Separate Meetings Model

**Structure:**
- **Meeting 1: Lord's Supper Meeting** (Weekly, Sunday morning)
  - Primary purpose: Breaking bread
  - Focus: Remembrance of Christ's death
  - Participatory but focused on Supper
  - No general doctrinal teaching
  - Reverent, Christ-centered

- **Meeting 2: Teaching/Participatory Meeting** (Weekly or bi-weekly, different time)
  - Primary purpose: Mutual edification
  - Focus: Teaching, doctrine, spiritual gifts
  - "Each one has" pattern
  - Doctrinal teaching welcome
  - Open participation

**Advantages:**
- Clear separation of purposes
- No dilution of either
- Allows extended time for each
- Maintains reverence in Lord's Supper
- Enables full participation in teaching meeting

**Challenges:**
- Requires two separate gatherings
- May reduce attendance at one
- Need to maintain unity between meetings

**Biblical Support:**
- 1 Corinthians 11 & 14 - Could be different meetings
- Acts 2:42-47 - Could be interpreted as different activities

**Early Church Precedent:**
- Tertullian - Eucharist (morning) separate from agape feast (evening)
- Hippolytus - Clear distinction between Eucharist and meal

**Modern Examples:**
- Some Brethren considering this
- Some house churches use this pattern

---

### Solution 3: Integrated with Clear Guidelines Model

**Structure:**
- **Single Meeting** with clear boundaries and guidelines
- **Lord's Supper Time:** Focused, reverent, Christ-centered
  - Clear boundaries: Focus on Lord's Supper, not general doctrine
  - Participatory but focused (hymns, prayers, Scripture related to Christ's death)
  - Breaking bread and sharing cup

- **Teaching Time:** Open for doctrine, teaching, spiritual gifts
  - Clear boundaries: After Lord's Supper, or designated time
  - "Each one has" pattern
  - Doctrinal teaching welcome
  - Mutual edification

**Guidelines (from SFMI model):**
- Time limits (10-15 min max, 5 min better)
- Focus on God, not self
- Discerning God's agenda for meeting
- "Oracles of God" - accurate teaching
- Order maintained ("decently and in order")

**Advantages:**
- Single meeting - simpler
- Clear boundaries prevent dilution
- Allows both reverence and participation
- Maintains unity in one gathering

**Challenges:**
- Requires clear guidelines and boundaries
- Need discipline to maintain focus
- May still feel tension between parts

**Biblical Support:**
- Acts 2:42-47 - Integrated experience
- Acts 20:7 - Breaking bread meeting included teaching
- 1 Corinthians 11 & 14 - Could be same meeting with different aspects

**Early Church Precedent:**
- Justin Martyr - Integrated Sunday meeting
- Didache - Prophets can contribute after communion

**Modern Examples:**
- SFMI - Open meetings with guidelines
- Some Brethren - Integrated with focus on Supper

---

### Solution 4: Sequential Structure Model

**Structure:**
- **Lord's Supper First** (30-45 minutes)
  - Reverent, focused remembrance
  - Breaking bread and sharing cup
  - Participatory but focused on Christ's death
  - Natural flow from remembrance to building up

- **Then Participatory Worship** (45-60 minutes)
  - Open for teaching, doctrine, spiritual gifts
  - "Each one has" pattern
  - Mutual edification
  - Natural progression from remembrance to building up

**Advantages:**
- Natural flow - remembrance leads to building up
- Clear sequence
- Maintains reverence in Lord's Supper
- Enables full participation afterward
- Single meeting

**Challenges:**
- May feel like two parts
- Need clear transition
- Some may leave after Lord's Supper

**Biblical Support:**
- Acts 20:7 - Breaking bread, then teaching
- 1 Corinthians 11 & 14 - Could be sequential
- Brethren teaching - Supper leads to worship

**Early Church Precedent:**
- Justin Martyr - Reading, teaching, prayer, then Eucharist (could be reversed)
- Brethren - Supper leads to worship

**Modern Examples:**
- Some Brethren - Sequential flow
- Some house churches - This pattern

### Recommended Approach: Two-Part Meeting Model

**Based on Synthesis:**

**Why This Model:**
1. **Honors both** - Reverent Lord's Supper AND open participatory worship
2. **Clear boundaries** - Prevents dilution of either
3. **Addresses your need** - Allows doctrinal teaching in appropriate time
4. **Maintains unity** - Same meeting, just two parts
5. **Biblical support** - Could be how 1 Corinthians 11 & 14 work
6. **Historical precedent** - Early church had both patterns
7. **Practical** - Works in practice (SFMI, some Brethren)

**Structure:**

**Part 1: Focused Lord's Supper** (30-45 minutes)
- **Purpose:** Remember Christ's death
- **Focus:** Christ-centered, reverent
- **Content:** 
  - Hymns about Christ's death
  - Prayers of thanksgiving
  - Scripture related to Lord's Supper
  - Breaking bread and sharing cup
  - Worship flowing from remembrance
- **Boundaries:** 
  - Focus on Lord's Supper, not general doctrine
  - No doctrinal teaching about healing, etc.
  - Keep focus on Christ's death

**Transition:** 
- Brief pause (2-3 minutes)
- Prayer or song
- Clear announcement: "Now we move to a time of mutual edification"

**Part 2: Open Participatory Worship** (45-60 minutes)
- **Purpose:** Mutual edification, teaching, doctrine
- **Focus:** Building up the body
- **Content:**
  - Doctrinal teaching (e.g., doctrine of healing)
  - "Each one has" pattern
  - Prophecy, teaching, testimonies, questions
  - Spiritual gifts exercised
  - Mutual exhortation
- **Guidelines (from SFMI):**
  - Time limits (10-15 min max, 5 min better)
  - Focus on God, not self
  - Discerning God's agenda
  - "Oracles of God" - accurate teaching
  - Order maintained

---

### Alternative: Separate Meetings Model

**If Two-Part Meeting Doesn't Work:**

**Meeting 1: Lord's Supper Meeting** (Weekly, Sunday morning)
- Primary purpose: Breaking bread
- Focus: Remembrance
- Participatory but focused
- No general doctrinal teaching

**Meeting 2: Teaching/Participatory Meeting** (Weekly or bi-weekly, different time)
- Primary purpose: Mutual edification
- Focus: Teaching, doctrine, spiritual gifts
- "Each one has" pattern
- Doctrinal teaching welcome

**Advantages:**
- Complete separation
- No dilution
- Extended time for each

**Challenges:**
- Two separate gatherings
- May reduce attendance
- Need to maintain unity

## PART 7: ADDRESSING COMMON CHALLENGES

### Challenge 1: Maintaining Focus in Lord's Supper

**Problem:** People want to share doctrinal things during Lord's Supper time

**Solution:**
- Clear boundaries and guidelines
- Gentle redirection if needed
- Reminder of purpose: "We're here to remember Christ's death"
- Save doctrinal teaching for Part 2 or separate meeting

---

### Challenge 2: Ensuring Participation in Part 2

**Problem:** People may not participate in Part 2

**Solution:**
- Clear invitation: "Now we have time for mutual edification"
- Encourage participation
- Make it welcoming, not intimidating
- Start with easier contributions (testimonies, questions)

---

### Challenge 3: Time Management

**Problem:** Meetings may go too long

**Solution:**
- Time limits (10-15 min max, 5 min better)
- Clear start and end times
- Prioritize most important contributions
- Can continue in fellowship time

---

### Challenge 4: Maintaining Unity

**Problem:** Two parts or two meetings may feel divided

**Solution:**
- Emphasize unity: "We're one body"
- Maintain fellowship between parts/meetings
- Don't let structure cause division
- Remember: Love and unity are more important than structure

---

### Challenge 5: Disagreements About Structure

**Problem:** People may disagree about which model to use

**Solution:**
- **Love and unity first** - More important than being "right"
- **Seek consensus** - Not majority rule
- **Be flexible** - Try different models, see what works
- **Remember principles** - Love, unity, remembrance, mutual edification
