## Part 2: Main Analysis (Using SCAR Quadrilateral)

### Chapter 1: Scripture

**Question:** What does the Bible say about meeting structure, Lord's Supper, and participatory worship?

**Key Passages:**
- 1 Corinthians 11:17-34 (Lord's Supper)
- 1 Corinthians 14:26-40 (Participatory worship - "each one has")
- Acts 2:42-47 (Daily breaking of bread)
- Acts 20:7 (Breaking bread on first day)
- Hebrews 10:24-25 (Mutual exhortation)
- Ephesians 5:19-20 (Mutual participation)
- Colossians 3:16 (Teaching and admonishing one another)

**Key Questions:**
- Are 1 Corinthians 11 and 14 describing the same meeting or different meetings?
- What is the relationship between Lord's Supper and participatory worship?
- Is there biblical precedent for separating or integrating these?

**See:** [[01-Chapter-Scripture|Chapter 1: Scripture]]

### Chapter 4: Antiquity (Pre-325 AD)

**Question:** How did the early church structure meetings with Lord's Supper and participatory worship?

**Key Sources:**
- Pliny the Younger (c. 112 AD) - Two meetings: before dawn and later meal
- Justin Martyr (c. 150 AD) - Sunday meeting structure: reading, exhortation, prayer, Eucharist
- Tertullian (c. 197 AD) - Agape feast with participatory elements
- Didache (c. 50-120 AD) - Thanksgiving prayers, prophets
- Hippolytus (c. 215 AD) - Apostolic Tradition, meeting structure
- Acts 2:42-47 - Daily breaking of bread with teaching and fellowship

**See:** [[04-Chapter-Antiquity|Chapter 4: Antiquity]]

### Chapter 7: Conclusion - Love and Unity

**The Ultimate Priority:**
- Jesus's command: "Love one another as I have loved you" (John 13:34-35)
- Jesus's prayer: "That they may all be one" (John 17:20-23)
- Without love, nothing else matters (1 Corinthians 13)
- Structure serves love and unity, not the other way around

**Implications:**
- Love must guide all decisions about meeting structure
- Unity must be preserved even when disagreeing about structure
- Love covers a multitude of sins (1 Peter 4:8)
- The ultimate test: Does it promote love and unity?

**Future Research Directions:**
- What we have learned so much, but there is so much more
- Questions that remain unanswered
- Further directions for research and practice
- Acknowledgment that the study is never truly finished
- Invitation to continue the journey together
