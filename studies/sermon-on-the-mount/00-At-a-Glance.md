# The Sermon on the Mount: At a Glance

**The question:** Is the Sermon on the Mount (Matthew 5--7) for believers today to follow?

**Key Scripture:** Matthew 28:19--20

> "Go ye therefore, and teach all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Ghost: Teaching them to observe all things whatsoever I have commanded you: and, lo, I am with you alway, even unto the end of the world. Amen."
> --- Matthew 28:19--20 (KJV)

---

## SCAR Summary

| Component | Key Finding |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Scripture** | The Great Commission (Matt 28:20) commands teaching "all things" Jesus commanded to "all nations" until "the end of the world." The Sermon is the largest block of Jesus's commands in Matthew. James, Paul, and Peter echo it throughout their epistles without dispensational qualification. The word "fulfil" (*plēroō*) in Matt 5:17 has multiple valid readings, but the six antitheses clearly intensify rather than abolish the law. The kingdom of heaven is both present ("theirs IS," 5:3) and future ("thy kingdom COME," 6:10). |
| **Christocentrism** | Jesus lived every demand of the Sermon --- turned the other cheek, loved his enemies, prayed for persecutors, gave without condition, owned nothing. He claimed authority over the Torah itself ("but I say unto you") and promised the Holy Spirit as the enabler of obedience. His governing priorities are love (John 13:34--35) and unity (John 17:20--23). |
| **Antiquity** | From the Didache (c. 70--100 AD) through Augustine (c. 394 AD), every early church writer who addresses the Sermon applies it to believers in his own time. The Didache uses it for baptismal catechesis. Justin Martyr presents it to the Roman emperor as actual Christian practice. Tertullian refuses military service on its basis. No pre-Nicene writer treats the Sermon as intended for a future dispensation. |
| **Reality** | Four major views exist: Literal Obedience (Anabaptist), Kingdom Ethics (mainstream evangelical/Reformed), Law's Mirror (Lutheran), and Future Kingdom (classical dispensational). Views 1 and 2, representing the vast majority of Christians across history, agree the Sermon is for all believers. Progressive dispensationalists have largely abandoned the "not for the church" reading. |

---

## Major Views

- **Literal Obedience (Anabaptist):** The Sermon contains direct commands from Christ to be obeyed without qualification --- non-resistance, refusal of oaths, enemy-love, simplicity of life.
- **Kingdom Ethics (Mainstream Evangelical):** The Sermon is binding on all believers, to be obeyed in the power of the Holy Spirit within the already/not-yet tension of the inaugurated kingdom.
- **Law's Mirror (Lutheran):** The Sermon's intentionally impossible demands expose human sinfulness and drive the hearer to dependence on Christ's grace.
- **Future Kingdom (Classical Dispensational):** The Sermon was addressed to Jewish disciples about the coming messianic kingdom and is not the church's direct marching orders.

---

## Common Ground

All four views agree: Jesus speaks with supreme authority; the Christian life requires transformation; the Sermon addresses the heart, not merely external behavior; love is the governing principle; grace and obedience are complementary, not contradictory; and hearing alone is not enough (Matt 7:24--27).

---

## For Every Believer

The Sermon on the Mount was given by a Teacher who expected his students to do the homework. Whatever theological framework you bring to it, the Sermon's own conclusion is clear: hear and DO. The one who hears and does builds on rock. The one who hears and does not builds on sand.

> "Therefore whosoever heareth these sayings of mine, and doeth them, I will liken him unto a wise man, which built his house upon a rock."
> --- Matthew 7:24 (KJV)
