# Chapter 2: Christocentrism --- Jesus, the True King on the True Throne

## Connecting to Chapter 1

Chapter 1's Scripture analysis identified several open questions that the Christocentric lens can help address:
- Why does Jesus identify Himself specifically as the one with "the sharp sword with two edges" when addressing Pergamum?
- What does Jesus's commendation of faithfulness amid Satan's throne tell us about His expectations for believers in hostile environments?
- Why does Jesus give Antipas His own title (*ho martus ho pistos*)?
- How do the promises to overcomers (hidden manna, white stone, new name) connect to Jesus's broader teaching?

## What Jesus Taught

### The True Throne Versus the Counterfeit

Jesus's letter to Pergamum is fundamentally a throne-room confrontation. Satan has a throne in Pergamum; Jesus sits on the true throne (Revelation 4-5). The letter declares that Jesus is aware of the counterfeit throne, names it for what it is, and asserts His supreme authority over it.

> "All power is given unto me in heaven and in earth."
> --- Matthew 28:18 (KJV)

Jesus's authority is not diminished by the existence of Satan's throne. Rather, the letter reveals that Jesus knows *exactly* where Satan's power is concentrated and that He will address it directly --- "I will come unto thee quickly, and will fight against them with the sword of my mouth" (Revelation 2:16).

### The Sword of the True Judge

> "For the word of God is quick, and powerful, and sharper than any twoedged sword, piercing even to the dividing asunder of soul and spirit, and of the joints and marrow, and is a discerner of the thoughts and intents of the heart."
> --- Hebrews 4:12 (KJV)

The Roman proconsul at Pergamum held the *ius gladii* --- the right of the sword, the power of capital punishment over the province of Asia. Jesus claims the greater sword. The implication is that the earthly authority that condemned Antipas to death did so under a delegated and ultimately counterfeit authority, while Jesus holds the true judicial power.

### Faithfulness Unto Death

> "Fear none of those things which thou shalt suffer: behold, the devil shall cast some of you into prison, that ye may be tried; and ye shall have tribulation ten days: be thou faithful unto death, and I will give thee a crown of life."
> --- Revelation 2:10 (KJV)

While addressed to Smyrna, this principle illuminates the Pergamum situation. Antipas embodied this call: he was faithful unto death, and Jesus commemorates him by name. The Christocentric principle is that faithfulness to Jesus may cost everything --- and that Jesus remembers every name.

## What Jesus Demonstrated

### Jesus Before Earthly Thrones

Jesus Himself stood before the throne of earthly power:

> "Then said Pilate unto him, Speakest thou not unto me? knowest thou not that I have power to crucify thee, and have power to release thee? Jesus answered, Thou couldest have no power at all against me, except it were given thee from above."
> --- John 19:10-11 (KJV)

This is the paradigm for Antipas and every Christian who stands before the enthroned power of Satan operating through earthly authority. The power to kill belongs ultimately to God, not to the proconsul, not to the emperor, and not to the altar of Zeus.

### Jesus Names His Witnesses

The deeply personal nature of Jesus's commendation --- "Antipas was my faithful martyr" --- reveals something about Jesus's character. He does not say "one of your members was killed." He names the man. He calls him *mine*. He uses His own title for him.

> "I am the good shepherd, and know my sheep, and am known of mine."
> --- John 10:14 (KJV)

> "Are not two sparrows sold for a farthing? and one of them shall not fall on the ground without your Father. But the very hairs of your head are all numbered."
> --- Matthew 10:29-30 (KJV)

## Jesus's Priorities: Love and Unity

> "A new commandment I give unto you, That ye love one another; as I have loved you, that ye also love one another."
> --- John 13:34 (KJV)

> "That they all may be one; as thou, Father, art in me, and I in thee, that they also may be one in us: that the world may believe that thou hast sent me."
> --- John 17:21 (KJV)

The Pergamum letter balances commendation with rebuke. Jesus commends the church's faithfulness and refusal to deny His name --- but rebukes them for tolerating false teaching (the doctrines of Balaam and the Nicolaitanes). Love without truth is not love; faithfulness to Jesus's name must include faithfulness to His teaching. Yet the call to repentance (v.16) is itself an act of love --- Jesus warns before He judges.

## The Kingdom in Enemy Territory

The Pergamum letter reveals a key Christocentric principle: **the kingdom of God advances in the very seat of the enemy's power.** The church in Pergamum did not relocate. They were not told to flee. They were commended for holding fast *right where Satan's throne was.* This aligns with Jesus's teaching:

> "The kingdom of heaven is like unto leaven, which a woman took, and hid in three measures of meal, till the whole was leavened."
> --- Matthew 13:33 (KJV)

The presence of the church in Pergamum, bearing witness at the foot of Satan's throne, is itself an act of spiritual warfare and kingdom advance.

## Summary

The Christocentric lens reveals that the Pergamum letter is about the confrontation of two thrones, two swords, and two authorities. Jesus claims ultimate sovereignty over the very city where Satan is enthroned. He remembers His faithful by name. He expects His church to stand firm in enemy territory while also maintaining doctrinal purity. The promises to overcomers --- hidden manna, white stone, new name --- are direct answers to the specific deprivations that faithfulness in Pergamum would bring.
