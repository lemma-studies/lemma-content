# The Angel of the Lord: At a Glance

**The question:** Who is the "Angel of the LORD" in the Hebrew Bible --- a created angelic being, a manifestation of the Father, or the pre-incarnate Son of God?

**Key Scripture:** Exodus 3:2, 4

> *"And the angel of the LORD appeared unto him in a flame of fire out of the midst of a bush: and he looked, and, behold, the bush burned with fire, and the bush was not consumed... And when the LORD saw that he turned aside to see, God called unto him out of the midst of the bush, and said, Moses, Moses. And he said, Here am I."*
> --- Exodus 3:2, 4 (KJV)

---

## SCAR Summary

| Component | Key Finding |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Scripture** | The Angel of the LORD speaks as God, accepts worship, forgives sins, and is called God by those who meet him --- yet is also sent *by* God. The identification and distinction coexist in the same passages, sometimes in the same verse. Key Hebrew terms (*go'el*, redeemer; *shem*, "my name is in him") point to a figure who carries the divine nature, not merely a divine message. |
| **Christocentrism** | The New Testament identifies Christ with the visible God of the Old Testament: "that Rock was Christ" (1 Cor 10:4), Isaiah saw Christ's glory (John 12:41), and the Father has never been seen (John 1:18). The Hebrews 1-2 counterargument --- that Christ is superior to angels --- presents a genuine tension that serious interpreters handle differently. |
| **Antiquity** | The pre-Nicene church (Justin, Irenaeus, Tertullian, Novatian, Eusebius) near-unanimously identified the Angel with the pre-incarnate Son. Jewish sources reflect a parallel awareness: Philo's Logos as "second God," the Targum Memra substitutions, and the Talmud's warning against "two powers in heaven." Augustine broke with this consensus after Nicaea to close a door the Arians had exploited. |
| **Reality** | Four views are held today: Christophany (pre-incarnate Christ), Created Angel, Theophany of the Father, and Literary Device. The SCAR priority tilts toward Christophany but does not settle the question. All views agree the figure is extraordinary and exercises divine prerogatives. |

---

## Major Views

- **Christophany (Pre-incarnate Christ):** The Son of God appearing in visible form before the incarnation --- the dominant pre-Nicene view and the majority evangelical position today.
- **Created Angel:** A supreme angelic being authorized to speak and act in God's name under the Jewish agency principle (*shaliah*).
- **Theophany of the Father:** God himself (or any person of the Trinity) manifesting through a created medium --- the Augustinian and Eastern Orthodox reading.
- **Literary Device:** A narrative convention for describing direct encounters with the divine, not a distinct being.

---

## Common Ground

All views agree that the Angel of the LORD is no ordinary figure. He exercises divine prerogatives --- forgiveness, redemption, creation --- that no other angel in Scripture possesses. Every passage reveals a God who draws near to people in crisis: to the rejected, the obscure, the frightened, and the barren. The tension between identification and distinction is a feature of the text itself, not a problem any single view has fully resolved.

---

## For Every Believer

Whatever you conclude about the Angel's identity, the passages themselves are invitations to wonder, not ammunition for arguments. God came down --- to Hagar in her tears, to Moses in his obscurity, to Gideon in his fear. He draws near still.

> *"For now we see through a glass, darkly; but then face to face: now I know in part; but then shall I know even as also I am known."*
> --- 1 Corinthians 13:12 (KJV)
