# Daniel 9:24: The Seventy Weeks

*"Seventy weeks are determined upon thy people and upon thy holy city, to finish the transgression, and to make an end of sins, and to make reconciliation for iniquity, and to bring in everlasting righteousness, and to seal up the vision and prophecy, and to anoint the most Holy."* --- Daniel 9:24 (KJV)

## The Question

In the first year of Darius the Mede, Daniel was studying the prophecy of Jeremiah and realized that the seventy years of Babylonian exile were nearly complete. He fell to his knees in prayer and confession --- and the angel Gabriel appeared with an answer that went far beyond what Daniel had asked.

Instead of seventy years, Gabriel revealed seventy *weeks* --- literally "seventy sevens," or 490 years --- determined upon Israel and Jerusalem. Within this period, six staggering accomplishments would be achieved: transgression finished, sins ended, iniquity reconciled, everlasting righteousness brought in, vision and prophecy sealed, and the Most Holy anointed.

The passage is among the most studied and debated in all of Scripture. It has been called the "backbone of biblical prophecy." It names a timeline, a Messiah, a death, and a destruction. Christians have read it as pointing to Christ since the earliest centuries. Jewish commentators acknowledged its messianic import. Modern scholars argue over its arithmetic.

This study examines Daniel 9:24-27 through the SCAR Quadrilateral: Scripture, Christocentrism, Antiquity, and Reality. The goal is to present the evidence honestly, acknowledge where faithful interpreters disagree, and hold the center with conviction while holding the edges with humility.

## What Scripture Says

Gabriel's message is precise and structured. The seventy weeks divide into three periods: seven weeks (49 years), sixty-two weeks (434 years), and one final week (7 years). After the sixty-nine weeks, "Messiah the Prince" appears. Then the Messiah is "cut off, but not for himself" --- a death that benefits others, not himself. After the cutting off, "the people of the prince that shall come shall destroy the city and the sanctuary" (Dan 9:26).

The six accomplishments of verse 24 are extraordinary:

1. **"To finish the transgression"** --- to bring Israel's corporate rebellion to its culmination and resolution.
2. **"To make an end of sins"** --- to seal up or complete the sin-offering.
3. **"To make reconciliation for iniquity"** --- the Hebrew *kaphar*, the same word used for the atonement sacrifices.
4. **"To bring in everlasting righteousness"** --- a permanent righteousness, not the temporary covering of the Mosaic sacrifices.
5. **"To seal up the vision and prophecy"** --- to fulfill and validate prophetic revelation.
6. **"To anoint the most Holy"** --- to consecrate the Holy of Holies or the Holy One himself.

The text leaves several critical questions open. Which "commandment to restore and to build Jerusalem" (Dan 9:25) starts the clock? The text does not name the decree or the king. What happens in the seventieth week? The text describes covenant-confirming and sacrifice-ceasing, but the referent of "he" in verse 27 is debated. These ambiguities are not modern inventions --- they have been discussed since the earliest commentators.

## What Jesus Taught

Jesus did not quote Daniel 9:24 directly, but his ministry, death, and resurrection fulfill its terms with remarkable precision. He is the Messiah who appears at the appointed time. He is "cut off, but not for himself" --- dying for others on the cross. His death accomplishes reconciliation for iniquity (2 Cor 5:21), brings in everlasting righteousness (Rom 3:21-26), and seals up prophecy (Luke 24:44).

The New Testament identifies Jesus as the fulfillment of Daniel's prophecy. When Jesus began his public ministry, he announced "The time is fulfilled" (Mark 1:15) --- language that echoes the predetermined timeline of Daniel's seventy weeks. The early church understood his death and resurrection as the decisive turning point in the prophetic calendar.

The destruction of the temple in AD 70 --- "the people of the prince that shall come shall destroy the city and the sanctuary" --- occurred within a generation of Christ's death, exactly as the sequence in Daniel 9:26 predicts: Messiah cut off, *then* the city and sanctuary destroyed.

## What the Earliest Christians Practiced

The church fathers read Daniel 9:24-27 as a Messianic prophecy fulfilled in Christ. This was unanimous. What they disagreed about was the arithmetic.

Hippolytus of Rome (c. 200 AD) counted the weeks from the return under Ezra and Nehemiah, placing Christ's baptism and crucifixion within the final week. Julius Africanus (c. 220 AD) produced the most detailed early calculation, counting from Artaxerxes' twentieth year and arriving at the crucifixion under Tiberius. Eusebius of Caesarea (c. 300 AD) followed a similar approach but adjusted the numbers. Jerome (c. 407 AD), in his *Commentary on Daniel*, surveyed multiple Jewish and Christian interpretations and defended the Christological reading against pagan critics like Porphyry.

Jewish tradition acknowledged the messianic character of the passage. The Talmud records the expectation that the Messiah should have come during the Second Temple period (Sanhedrin 97b). Seder Olam Rabbah, the rabbinic chronology, compressed the Persian period --- scholars have debated whether this was to avoid a chronological alignment with Jesus of Nazareth.

The most important observation is that the early church agreed on what mattered --- Christ is the Messiah of Daniel 9 --- while acknowledging genuine difficulty with the precise calculations. They did not treat chronological disagreement as a test of orthodoxy.

## The Major Views Today

### View 1: Artaxerxes 20, Prophetic Years (Anderson/Hoehner)

The classic dispensational calculation. Sir Robert Anderson (1895) counted 69 weeks of 360-day "prophetic years" from Nisan 445 BC (Artaxerxes' twentieth year, per Nehemiah 2) to Palm Sunday AD 32. Harold Hoehner refined the calculation to AD 33. This yields exactly 173,880 days. The 70th week is typically placed in the future as a tribulation period. This view requires the 360-day year to be a divinely intended prophetic unit.

### View 2: Artaxerxes 7, Solar Years (SDA/Traditional)

Beginning from Artaxerxes' seventh year (458/457 BC, per Ezra 7), counting 483 solar years reaches AD 27 --- the approximate year of Jesus's baptism. The 70th week then runs from AD 27 to AD 34, with the crucifixion "in the midst of the week" (c. AD 31). This view uses standard solar years and places the entire seventy weeks in the past. It is held by Seventh-day Adventists and some Reformed interpreters.

### View 3: Revised Chronology (Eclipse-Based)

If Xerxes died in 475 BC (as indicated by the Babylonian tablet BM 32234 and NASA eclipse data), rather than the conventional 465 BC, then Artaxerxes' reign begins ten years earlier. His twentieth year shifts from 445 to 455/456 BC. This affects all calculations that depend on Artaxerxes' dates and is an active area of research. The Daniel 9:24 study includes original chronological analysis based on this evidence.

### View 4: Symbolic/Theological Reading

A minority position that takes the seventy weeks as theologically structured rather than arithmetically precise. The numbers signify completeness (70 x 7) rather than exact years. This view focuses on the six accomplishments and their fulfillment in Christ without requiring precise date-matching. It is held by some critical scholars and some theologians who see the passage's power in its theological claims rather than its calendar.

## What All Views Agree On

Despite significant disagreement on dates, calendars, and the 70th week, all Christian interpreters agree on the essentials:

- **Jesus Christ is the Messiah of Daniel 9:24-27.** The six accomplishments --- finishing transgression, ending sins, reconciling iniquity, bringing everlasting righteousness, sealing prophecy, and anointing the Most Holy --- are fulfilled in his person and work.
- **The prophecy validates the gospel.** God announced his redemptive plan centuries in advance and carried it out in history. This is evidence for the reliability of Scripture and the truth of the Christian message.
- **The Messiah was "cut off, but not for himself."** Christ's death was substitutionary --- for others, not for his own sins.
- **The destruction of the temple in AD 70 is part of the prophetic sequence.** After the Messiah is cut off, the city and sanctuary are destroyed.
- **The passage serves faith, worship, and mission.** Its purpose is to strengthen trust in God's faithfulness, magnify Christ, and support gospel proclamation --- not to fuel speculation or divide believers over secondary points.

## For the Reader

Daniel 9:24-27 is one of the most remarkable prophecies in Scripture. It names a timeline, a Messiah, a death, a destruction, and six cosmic accomplishments. For two thousand years, Christians have seen in it the fingerprint of God's plan, written centuries before Bethlehem and Calvary.

This study has attempted to present the evidence honestly and fairly. Where there is agreement, we have stated it clearly. Where there is disagreement, we have presented each view at its strongest, with its best advocates and its honest challenges.

The center holds: **Christ is the fulfillment.** The six accomplishments are achieved in his person and work. The prophecy validates the gospel. This is not negotiable.

The edges are open: the starting decree, the calendar system, the 70th week. These are legitimate questions where faithful interpreters disagree. Hold them with humility, discuss them with charity, and do not let them divide what the prophecy was meant to unite.
