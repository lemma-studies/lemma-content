## Connecting to Chapter 1

Chapter 1's Scripture analysis identified several open questions that the Christocentric lens can help address:

- Does Jesus specify the timing of the rapture relative to antichrist?
- What are Jesus's priorities when teaching about His return?
- How does love and unity factor into eschatological readiness?

## What Jesus Taught

### The Olivet Discourse (Matthew 24; Mark 13; Luke 21)

Jesus's most extensive eschatological teaching occurs on the Mount of Olives when His disciples ask about the destruction of the temple and "the sign of thy coming, and of the end of the world" (Matthew 24:3).

#### The Key Question

> "And as he sat upon the mount of Olives, the disciples came unto him privately, saying, Tell us, when shall these things be? and what shall be the sign of thy coming, and of the end of the world?"
> --- Matthew 24:3 (KJV)

Jesus responds with what has become known as the Olivet Discourse — His longest eschatological teaching.

#### False Christs and Deception

> "Take heed that no man deceive you. For many shall come in my name, saying, I am Christ; and shall deceive many."
> --- Matthew 24:4-5 (KJV)

Jesus explicitly warns about false christs appearing before His return. This is significant: antichrist-type figures (false christs) are a sign that the end is approaching, not that it has arrived.

#### The Sequence of Events

> "And ye shall hear of wars and rumours of wars: see that ye be not troubled: for all these things must come to pass, but the end is not yet. For nation shall rise against nation, and kingdom against kingdom: and there shall be famines, and pestilences, and earthquakes, in divers places. All these are the beginning of sorrows."
> --- Matthew 24:6-8 (KJV)

Jesus teaches that wars, famines, and earthquakes are the "beginning of sorrows" (birth pangs) — not the end itself. This suggests a sequence: these events precede the end.

#### Persecution and Endurance

> "Then shall they deliver you up to be afflicted, and shall kill you: and ye shall be hated of all nations for my name's sake. And then shall many be offended, and shall betray one another, and shall hate one another. And many false prophets shall rise, and shall deceive many. And because iniquity shall abound, the love of many shall wax cold. But he that shall endure unto the end, the same shall be saved."
> --- Matthew 24:9-13 (KJV)

Believers will face persecution, betrayal, and hatred. Yet the call is to endure to the end — whatever the timing of that end.

#### The Gospel to All Nations

> "And this gospel of the kingdom shall be preached in all the world for a witness unto all nations; and then shall the end come."
> --- Matthew 24:14 (KJV)

A crucial marker: the gospel must reach all nations before the end comes. This creates a condition (or at least a marker) for the end.

#### The Abomination of Desolation

> "When ye therefore shall see the abomination of desolation, spoken of by Daniel the prophet, stand in the holy place, (whoso readeth, let him understand:) Then let them which be in Judaea flee into the mountains..."
> --- Matthew 24:15-16 (KJV)

Jesus references Daniel's prophecy of the abomination of desolation — a future antichrist action that triggers the need for flight.

#### The Great Tribulation

> "For then shall be great tribulation, such as was not since the beginning of the world to this time, no, nor ever shall be. And except those days should be shortened, there should no flesh be saved: but for the elect's sake those days shall be shortened."
> --- Matthew 24:21-22 (KJV)

A "great tribulation" is coming — worse than anything previously. Yet for the elect's sake, it will be shortened.

#### The Sign of the Son of Man

> "And then shall appear the sign of the Son of man in heaven: and then shall all the tribes of the earth mourn, and they shall see the Son of man coming in the clouds of heaven with power and great glory. And he shall send his angels with a great sound of a trumpet, and they shall gather together his elect from the four winds, from one end of heaven to the other."
> --- Matthew 24:30-31 (KJV)

Jesus describes His return with trumpet, gathering of elect — similar to 1 Thessalonians 4:16-17 language. (Whether this gathering is the church's rapture or a distinct second-coming assembly is disputed: pre-tribulational interpreters read the Olivet Discourse's frame as Israel rather than the church, and take the "one taken, one left" of Matthew 24:40-42 as removal *in judgment* on the Noah pattern, not the rapture — Pettegrew, TMSJ 13 [2002]: 173-90; Mayhue, TMSJ 13 [2002]: 250.)

### Two Escape Texts in Jesus's Own Words

Two sayings of Jesus carry the strongest escape language, and both readings deserve full strength:

> "Watch ye therefore, and pray always, that ye may be accounted worthy to escape all these things that shall come to pass, and to stand before the Son of man."
> --- Luke 21:36 (KJV)

Pre-tribulation (and partial-rapture) readers hear a promise of *removal before* "all these things"; the alternative reading takes "escape" (ἐκφεύγω) as *coming safely through* them --- the verse ends not in absence but in *standing before the Son of man*, and the same discourse has just said "your redemption draweth nigh" to people watching the signs (21:28). The verse promises escape; it does not specify the mode.

> "In my Father's house are many mansions... I will come again, and receive you unto myself; that where I am, there ye may be also."
> --- John 14:2-3 (KJV)

This is the foundation of the pre-trib "bridegroom" argument (its fullest form in Renald Showers, *Maranatha: Our Lord, Come!*, 1995): Jesus comes *to take believers to the Father's house*, which reads most naturally as a removal to heaven rather than a descent to reign. The alternative reading notes that the promise's center is presence --- "that where I am, there ye may be also" --- which every view affirms, and that the passage names no tribulation timing at all.

### What Jesus Did NOT Say

Strikingly, Jesus does NOT:

- Specify a pre-tribulation rapture
- Promise escape from tribulation *unambiguously* --- Luke 21:36 is the closest, and its mode (removal vs. preservation) is precisely what the traditions dispute
- Give a detailed timeline of rapture vs. antichrist
- Condemn those who hold different eschatological views

What Jesus DID emphasize:

### The Parable of the Ten Virgins (Matthew 25:1-13)

> "Then shall the kingdom of heaven be likened unto ten virgins, which took their lamps, and went forth to meet the bridegroom. And five of them were wise, and five were foolish. They that were foolish took their lamps, and took no oil with them: But the wise took oil in their vessels with their lamps. While the bridegroom tarried, they all slumbered and slept. And at midnight there was a cry made, Behold, the bridegroom cometh; go ye out to meet him. Then all those virgins arose, and trimmed their lamps. And the foolish said unto the wise, Give us of your oil; for our lamps are gone out. But the wise answered, saying, Not so; lest there be not enough for us and you: but go ye rather to them that sell, and buy for yourselves. And while they went to buy, the bridegroom came; and they that were ready went in with him to the marriage: and the door was shut. Afterward came also the other virgins, saying, Lord, Lord, open to us. But he answered and said, Verily I say unto you, I know you not. Watch therefore, for ye know neither the day nor the hour wherein the Son of man cometh."
> --- Matthew 25:1-13 (KJV)

**Key lessons:**
- The bridegroom "tarried" (delayed) — readiness matters more than timing
- Both wise and foolish virgins were sleeping when the cry came
- Being ready ("oil in their vessels") is essential
- The master "knows not" the day or hour

### The Faithful Servant vs. Evil Servant (Matthew 24:45-51)

> "Who then is a faithful and wise servant, whom his lord hath made ruler over his household, to give them meat in due season? Blessed is that servant, whom his lord when he cometh shall find so doing. Verily I say unto you, That he shall make him ruler over all his goods. But and if that evil servant shall say in his heart, My lord delayeth his coming; And shall begin to smite his fellowservants, and to eat and drink with the drunken; The lord of that servant shall come in a day when he looketh not for him, and in an hour that he is not aware of, And shall cut him asunder, and appoint him his portion with the hypocrites: there shall be weeping and gnashing of teeth."
> --- Matthew 24:45-51 (KJV)

**Key lessons:**
- Faithfulness during the "delay" is what matters
- Those who assume the master is "delayeth" may become abusive
- The return is sudden and unexpected

## What Jesus Demonstrated

### Jesus's Own Return Pattern

Consider how Jesus left:
- Ascended bodily from the Mount of Olives (Acts 1:9-11)
- Angels promised He would return the same way
- He sits at the Father's right hand until His enemies are made His footstool (Hebrews 10:12-13)

### The Spirit's Present Work

Jesus did not leave believers without help:
- The Holy Spirit indwells believers now (John 14:16-17)
- The Spirit is the "earnest" or "down payment" of our inheritance (Ephesians 1:13-14)
- The Spirit empowers witness, not escape from tribulation

## Jesus's Priorities: Love and Unity

> "A new commandment I give unto you, That ye love one another; as I have loved you, that ye also love one another."
> --- John 13:34 (KJV)

> "That they all may be one; as thou, Father, art in me, and I in thee, that they also may be one in us: that the world may believe that thou hast sent me."
> --- John 17:21 (KJV)

### Application to This Question

**Love test:** Does any view on rapture timing undermine love for brothers and sisters who disagree? The pre-tribulation view sometimes creates division ("you're not really saved if you don't believe this"). Post-tribulation views sometimes judge pre-tribulation believers as "escapist." Both attitudes violate Jesus's love command.

**Unity test:** Can believers with different eschatological views maintain genuine fellowship? Absolutely — when they prioritize Christ over speculation.

**Watchfulness:** Jesus's consistent call is to be watchful, ready, faithful — regardless of whether we believe in pre, mid, or post-tribulation rapture.

## Summary

The Christocentric lens reveals:

1. **Jesus did not specify exact timing** of rapture relative to antichrist
2. **Jesus emphasized readiness** through faithfulness, not date-setting
3. **Jesus warned about deception** — false christs would arise before the end
4. **Jesus prioritized love and unity** — these matter more than eschatological speculation
5. **Jesus taught endurance** — "he that endureth to the end, the same shall be saved"

The timing of rapture relative to antichrist remains an open question in Scripture. Jesus's teaching does not settle it — but His priorities do: watch, be ready, love one another.
