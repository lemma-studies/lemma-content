> "Surely he hath borne our griefs, and carried our sorrows: yet we did esteem him stricken, smitten of God, and afflicted. But he was wounded for our transgressions, he was bruised for our iniquities: the chastisement of our peace was upon him; and with his stripes we are healed."
> --- Isaiah 53:4--5 (KJV)

## The Foundation of the Inquiry

In the SCAR framework, Scripture holds the highest authority. Before examining what Jesus demonstrated (Christocentrism), what the early church believed (Antiquity), or what believers experience today (Reality), we must ask the most fundamental question: **What does the Bible actually say?**

This chapter examines the biblical texts at the center of the healing-in-the-atonement debate. The question is deceptively simple:

**When Isaiah 53:5 says "with his stripes we are healed," does that healing include physical healing --- or is it exclusively spiritual?**

The answer depends on how we read the Hebrew words behind our English translations, how we understand the context of Isaiah's Fourth Servant Song, and how we interpret the New Testament authors who quote this passage. Faithful scholars have reached different conclusions, and this chapter presents the evidence that informs each position.

## The Central Passages

Four biblical texts form the core of this debate. Each is examined in detail in its own section:

### Isaiah 52:13--53:12 --- The Fourth Servant Song

This is the primary passage --- the origin of the phrase "by his stripes we are healed." The Fourth Servant Song describes the suffering, death, and vindication of the Servant of the LORD. Verses 4--5 are the epicenter of the debate, and the Hebrew words behind the English translations are genuinely ambiguous: they *can* refer to physical sickness and healing, and they *can* refer to spiritual grief and restoration. How translators and interpreters handle this ambiguity shapes the entire discussion.

### Matthew 8:16--17 --- The Fulfillment Citation

> "When the even was come, they brought unto him many that were possessed with devils: and he cast out the spirits with his word, and healed all that were sick: That it might be fulfilled which was spoken by Esaias the prophet, saying, Himself took our infirmities, and bare our sicknesses."
> --- Matthew 8:16--17 (KJV)

Matthew quotes Isaiah 53:4 in the context of Jesus physically healing the sick. This is the strongest New Testament evidence that Isaiah 53 includes physical healing. But interpreters disagree on *how* Matthew is applying the passage --- is he saying the atonement provides healing, or is he showing a broader pattern of the Servant's identification with human suffering?

[Read the full analysis of Matthew 8:16--17 &rarr;](/01-scripture/matthew-8/)

### 1 Peter 2:24 --- The Spiritual Application

> "Who his own self bare our sins in his own body on the tree, that we, being dead to sins, should live unto righteousness: by whose stripes ye were healed."
> --- 1 Peter 2:24 (KJV)

Peter quotes Isaiah 53:5 in a context that is explicitly about sin and righteousness --- not physical sickness. For many interpreters, this settles the question: Peter understood "healed" as spiritual restoration. But others argue that Peter is applying *one dimension* of a passage that has multiple dimensions, and that his spiritual application does not exclude the physical meaning found in Matthew 8:17.

[Read the full analysis of 1 Peter 2:24 &rarr;](/01-scripture/1-peter-2/)

### Related Passages

Several other biblical texts inform the discussion, including:

- **Psalm 103:2--3** (KJV): "Bless the LORD, O my soul, and forget not all his benefits: Who forgiveth all thine iniquities; who healeth all thy diseases." --- Forgiveness and healing paired together.
- **Exodus 15:26** (KJV): "...for I am the LORD that healeth thee." --- God as Healer (Yahweh Rapha) as a covenant name.
- **James 5:14--16** (KJV): "Is any sick among you? let him call for the elders of the church... and the prayer of faith shall save the sick, and the Lord shall raise him up; and if he have committed sins, they shall be forgiven him." --- Physical healing and forgiveness of sins linked in practice.
- **Romans 8:22--23** (KJV): "For we know that the whole creation groaneth and travaileth in pain together until now. And not only they, but ourselves also, which have the firstfruits of the Spirit, even we ourselves groan within ourselves, waiting for the adoption, to wit, the redemption of our body." --- The "not yet" of bodily redemption.

## How This Chapter Is Organized

Each passage analysis follows a consistent structure:

1. **The full text in KJV** --- so the reader sees the actual words
2. **Cross-version comparison** --- how major translations render the key terms differently, and why it matters
3. **Word study** --- the Hebrew or Greek behind the English, with the full semantic range of each term
4. **Context** --- what comes before and after, and how the passage fits in its book
5. **The interpretive spectrum** --- every major reading of the passage, presented with its strongest evidence
6. **What is clear and what is debated** --- an honest summary separating settled ground from open questions

## A Note on Translation

One of the most important discoveries in this chapter is that **English translations differ significantly** on the key verses. The KJV renders the Hebrew words in Isaiah 53:4 as "griefs" and "sorrows." The NASB (2020) renders the same Hebrew words as "sicknesses" and "pains." Neither translation is wrong --- the Hebrew words genuinely carry both meanings. But the translation choice shapes what the reader sees, and therefore what the reader concludes.

This study uses the KJV as the primary translation, but consistently compares across the ESV, NASB (both 1995 and 2020 editions), LSB, and NIV. Where the Septuagint (LXX) --- the ancient Greek translation of the Old Testament --- differs from the Hebrew Masoretic Text, that difference is noted, because the New Testament authors sometimes quote from the LXX rather than the Hebrew.

## Scripture as the Starting Point

Whatever conclusion a reader reaches about physical healing in the atonement, it must begin here --- with what the text says in its original languages, in its literary context, and in the way the New Testament authors understood and applied it. The evidence is richer and more nuanced than any single proof text can capture. The pages that follow lay it out for the reader to examine.
