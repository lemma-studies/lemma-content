## Overview

This chapter examines **modern examples** of how Christian groups structure meetings to honor both the Lord's Supper and participatory worship. The "Reality" component of the SCAR Quadrilateral asks: "Can this be lived? Are there examples of faithful believers doing so?"

**Key Principle:** If an interpretation of Scripture leads to outcomes contrary to Christ's character, or if no one can actually live it out, we should question the interpretation. Conversely, when communities successfully practice a teaching, it validates the interpretation.

## PART 1: PLYMOUTH BRETHREN

### Background

**Founded:** 1820s in Plymouth, England  
**Key Figures:** John Nelson Darby, George Müller, Anthony Norris Groves  
**Current Status:** Global movement with thousands of assemblies

### Meeting Structure

**Primary Purpose: The Lord's Supper**

**Alfred P. Gibbs:**
> "The Lord's Supper, as we have already seen, is for all the Lord's people who are sound in life and doctrine. An examination of the practice of the early Church, as seen in the book of Acts, seems to indicate that brethren of a given community came together each Lord's day to show forth the Lord's death in the breaking of bread (Acts 20:7). **They came primarily, not to hear preaching, but to break the bread**, symbolic of the body of their Lord and Savior; and drink the cup, symbolic of His precious blood."

**C.H. Mackintosh:**
> "It should be the special aim of Christians, to show that the breaking of bread is their grand and primary object in coming together, on the first day of the week. They should show that it is not for preaching or teaching that they assemble, though teaching may be a happy adjunct, but that the breaking of bread is the leading object before their minds."

**Key Characteristics:**
- **Primary purpose:** Breaking bread, not preaching
- **Frequency:** Every Lord's Day (first day of the week)
- **Focus:** Remembering the Lord's death
- **Pattern:** Following Acts 20:7

---

### No Human Presidency

**William Kelly:**
> "Human presidency there is avoided in scripture. How precious therein to merge all else and have nothing before the soul but Himself Who died for us in infinite love! This the Lord (the night in which He was betrayed) commended to the saints."

**Alfred P. Gibbs:**
> "There is no visible head at this feast of remembrance, for none such is contemplated in Scripture. Christ, however, is there according to His promise: 'Where two or three are gathered together in My name, there am I in the midst of them,' and who would dare to take His place?"

**Key Characteristics:**
- No ordained minister presiding
- No human "head" at the table
- Christ Himself is the Host
- Rejection of clericalism

---

### Spirit-Led, Participatory Meetings

**Alfred P. Gibbs:**
> "Inasmuch as it is the Lord's supper, there is no one present who attempts to usurp the Lordship and authority of Christ by attempting to take His place, or arrange its program. **One after another of the brethren arise as the Spirit leads**--one with a hymn, another voicing the worship of the assembly, another with some Scripture exposition in keeping with the feast of remembrance. One rises to give thanks for the bread, and it is broken and passed so that all may break it and eat. Perhaps another rises to give thanks for the cup and it, in turn, is passed from one to the other."

**H.A. Ironside:**
> "It must be remembered that when we come together for the Lord's Supper, Christ is as truly present in our midst as He was among the first disciples two thousand years ago... The Holy Spirit will guide those gathered concerning prayers of worship, the spiritual tone, and portions of the Word of God which exalt the Lord Jesus Christ."

**Key Characteristics:**
- No pre-arranged program
- Spirit-led participation
- Multiple brothers may contribute
- Flexibility in order
- Silence is acceptable and meaningful
- Focus on Lord's Supper, but participatory

---

### Current Tension

**The Situation:**
- Weekly meeting is "kind of open" around the Lord's Supper
- People are free to speak BUT must focus on the Lord's Supper
- Doctrinal teaching (e.g., doctrine of healing) is not welcome during Lord's Supper time
- After meeting: snacks, singing, testimonies, prayer requests
- Still not a place for doctrinal sharing/learnings in "open meeting way"
- There's a prepared lesson with discussion
- Some want another segment ("two meetings") where they can "exercise" gifts and share doctrinal things

**The Challenge:**
- Need for focused, reverent remembrance of Christ's death
- Need for open participatory teaching and mutual edification
- How to honor both without diluting either?

**The Question:**
- Should there be a separate time for doctrinal teaching and "exercising gifts"?
- Or should the Lord's Supper meeting be more open to doctrinal teaching?
- How to balance reverence and participation?

---

### Brethren Approach to the Tension

**Strengths:**
- Lord's Supper as primary purpose (biblical)
- Participatory elements (biblical)
- Reverence maintained (important)
- Spirit-led approach (good)

**Challenges:**
- Tension between focus and openness
- Doctrinal teaching not fitting in current structure
- Some wanting more opportunity to share
- Need for balance

**Possible Solutions Being Considered:**
- "Two meetings" - Separate time for doctrinal teaching
- More openness in Lord's Supper meeting
- Better balance between reverence and participation

---

### SFMI Conference Orientation Script

**Source:** Conference Orientation Script provided by a brother at SFMI (November 2024)

This orientation script provides **practical, detailed guidelines** for how SFMI conducts open/participatory meetings. It demonstrates their actual implementation of participatory worship with biblical boundaries and practical wisdom.

#### Key Scriptural Foundations

**1. All Members Important (1 Corinthians 12):**
- All members of the Body of Christ are important
- God distributes a variety of gifts as He decides
- Foundation for participatory worship

**2. "Each One Has" Pattern (1 Corinthians 14:26):**
- "When you come together, each of you has a psalm, a teaching, a tongue, a revelation, an interpretation... let all be done for edification"
- Direct biblical basis for participatory meetings
- All members can contribute

**3. Gender Roles (1 Timothy 2:12):**
- "Do not permit a woman to teach or have authority over a man"
- Acknowledges Jesus valued women and children equally
- But God has set structure and restrictions on gatherings
- Practical application of complementarian theology

---

#### Practical Guidelines

**1. Testimonies:**
- **God's testimony, not man's** - Focus on what God did, not human details
- "Be short on human details and long on God's actions"
- Details of how bad the situation was focus attention on the person, not God
- Personal stories belong in fellowship before/after meetings, not in the meeting itself

**2. Speaking in Tongues:**
- **Scripture admonishes not to forbid speaking in tongues** (1 Corinthians 14:39)
- But requires interpretation: "If there be no interpreter, let him keep silence in the church; and let him speak to himself, and to God" (1 Corinthians 14:28)
- Balance between allowing gifts and maintaining order

**3. Teaching/Revelation:**
- **Men are free to bring teaching**
- Must "speak the Oracles (truths) of God" (1 Peter 4:11)
- "The spirit of the prophets are subject to the prophets" (1 Corinthians 14:32)
- Must be accurate to God's truth
- Must be discerning about "when" the Holy Spirit wants them to speak

**4. Discerning God's Agenda:**
- **God often has His own agenda for each meeting**
- Need to be sensitive to how the Spirit is leading
- Should not bring anything that distracts from God's agenda for that meeting
- "How is the Holy Spirit leading this meeting, right now?"
- Encourages brothers to be discerning about timing

**5. Time Management:**
- **"All can prophecy one by one"** (1 Corinthians 14:31)
- Many can speak, but in large assemblies, keep time short
- **10-15 minutes maximum at the mic**
- **5 minutes is better**
- Ensures others have opportunity to participate

**6. Value of Silence:**
- **"Silence is okay - for it is in those times that we hear God best"**
- Not every moment needs to be filled
- Allows space for God to speak
- Important reminder in participatory meetings

---

#### Meeting Structure

**First Open Meeting (Full Orientation):**
1. Opening prayer - ask God's blessing
2. Announcement: "Let us now sing for a bit and remind ourselves that silence is okay – for it is in those times that we hear God best"
3. Open participation begins
4. Generally includes: singing, prayer, testimony, teaching

**Second Open Meeting (Brief Reminder):**
- Brief summary for those who missed first night
- Reminder of key guidelines
- Emphasis on sensitivity to Spirit's leading

---

#### Key Insights

**1. Balance of Freedom and Order:**
- Participatory (all can contribute)
- But with biblical boundaries (gender roles, interpretation, discernment)
- Not chaos, but ordered participation

**2. Spirit-Led, Not Program-Driven:**
- Emphasis on discerning what God wants for this specific meeting
- Not just following a program
- Sensitivity to Holy Spirit's leading

**3. Edification Focus:**
- All contributions must be "for edification"
- Testimonies focus on God, not self
- Teaching must be "oracles of God"
- Everything serves to build up the body

**4. Practical Wisdom:**
- Time limits prevent domination by one person
- Silence valued as important
- Personal stories in fellowship, not meeting
- Balance between participation and order

**5. Regulative Principle in Practice:**
- All guidelines based on Scripture
- No human innovations
- Strict adherence to biblical patterns
- Distinguishes between God's commands and human traditions

## PART 5: EARLY PENTECOSTALS (1900s)

### Background

**Azusa Street Revival (1906-1909):**
- **Location:** 312 Azusa Street, Los Angeles, California
- **Leader:** William J. Seymour (African American Holiness Pentecostal preacher)
- **Significance:** Catalyst for global spread of Pentecostalism
- **Duration:** Peak years 1906-1909, but influence continues

**Key Figures:**
- **William J. Seymour** - Leader of Azusa Street Revival
- **Frank Bartleman** - Chronicler and participant
- **Charles Parham** - Early Pentecostal teacher (1901)
- **Agnes Ozman** - First to speak in tongues (1901)
- **Lucy Farrow** - Key figure in laying on of hands

**Reference:** See `Early-Pentecostal-Writings-1900s/` folder for detailed source files with complete quotes

---

### Meeting Structure: Completely Spirit-Led

**Frank Bartleman's Description:**
> "Brother Seymour generally sat behind two empty boxes, one on top of the other. He usually kept his head inside the top one during the meeting, in prayer. There was no pride there. The services ran almost continuously. Seeking souls could be found under the power almost any hour of the day or night. The place was never closed or empty. The people came to meet God – He was always there. Hence a continuous meeting. The meeting did not depend on the human leader."

**On No Human Program:**
> "No subjects or sermons were announced ahead of time, and no special speakers for such an hour."

**On Open Participation:**
> "Services began in the morning and continued for ten hours and more. There was no order of service, and usually no one was leading. 'Whoever was anointed with the message would stand and deliver it. It might be a man, woman, or child.'"

**Key Characteristics:**
- **No human program** - Led entirely by the Holy Spirit
- **Spontaneous** - All activities occurred as the Spirit led
- **Extended duration** - Meetings from 10 AM to near midnight, sometimes until 3:00 AM
- **Three daily services** - Morning, afternoon, and night
- **Continuous worship** - Services going almost around the clock

---

### Participatory Worship: "Each One Has" Pattern

**Activities During Meetings:**
- Spontaneous singing (a cappella, no instruments)
- Testimonies (anyone could share)
- Prayer (corporate and individual)
- Preaching (extemporaneous, Spirit-led)
- Speaking in tongues
- Prophecy
- Healing
- Laying on of hands
- People falling prostrate in prayer

**Frank Bartleman on Spontaneous Worship:**
> "Suddenly the Spirit would fall upon the congregation. God himself would give the altar call. Men would fall all over the house, like the slain in battle, or rush for the altar en masse to seek God. The scene often resembled a forest of fallen trees."

**Key:** Completely participatory - anyone could participate as the Spirit led.

---

### Women's Participation

**Women's Active Participation:**
- **Women Could Preach:** Women were encouraged to exercise spiritual gifts, including preaching
- **Women Prophesied:** Women spoke publicly by "prophesying" or sharing testimonies
- **Women Led Prayer:** Women led prayer meetings and delivered prayers
- **No Gender Restrictions:** The revival broke traditional gender barriers
- **Women in Leadership:** Women like Lucy Farrow were recognized as "anointed handmaidens"
- **Jennie Moore:** Among the first to speak in tongues at Bonnie Brae, later co-pastored with Seymour

**Significance:** The active participation of women in early Pentecostal meetings was unprecedented for the time.

---

### Racial Unity

**William Seymour:**
> "No instrument that God can use is rejected on account of color or dress or lack of education."

**The Apostolic Faith Newspaper:**
> "The color line was washed away in the blood."

**Los Angeles Times Report:**
> "A crowd of colored people and a sprinkling of whites compose the congregation."

> "Whites and blacks mix in a religious frenzy."

**Significance:** The Azusa Street Revival broke racial barriers unprecedented for the time.

---

### Lord's Supper/Communion

**Observance:**
- **Not Emphasized:** Specific accounts of communion at Azusa Street are scarce in primary sources
- **Focus on Spiritual Experiences:** The emphasis was on tongues, healing, prophecy rather than traditional sacraments
- **Less Liturgical:** Less attention to traditional liturgical practices like communion
- **Spirit-Focused:** The focus was on the immediate presence and work of the Holy Spirit

**Note:** While communion is not prominently featured in accounts, this does not mean it was never observed. The primary sources emphasize the spontaneous, Spirit-led experiences rather than structured liturgical practices.

---

### Key Principles

1. **No Human Program:** Everything led by the Holy Spirit
2. **Open Participation:** Anyone could participate as the Spirit led
3. **Spontaneous Structure:** No fixed order, completely free form
4. **Gender Inclusive:** Women actively participated and led
5. **Racial Inclusive:** "The color line was washed away in the blood"
6. **Bible-Centered:** "Measuring everything by the Word"
7. **Spirit-Focused:** Emphasis on immediate presence and work of the Holy Spirit

---

### Significance

**Why Early Pentecostals Matter:**
- **Demonstrates fully participatory worship** - "Each one has" pattern in practice
- **Shows Spirit-led meetings work** - No human program needed
- **Breaks barriers** - Racial and gender inclusivity
- **Global impact** - Spread Pentecostalism worldwide
- **Practical viability** - Shows participatory worship can work on large scale

**Key Insight:** Early Pentecostals practiced fully participatory worship with no human program, showing that Spirit-led, "each one has" meetings can work effectively.

## PART 6: OTHER MODERN EXAMPLES

### 1. New Testament Reformation Fellowship (NTRF)

**Background:**
- **Founded:** 1990s (exact date unclear)
- **Website:** ntrf.org
- **Theological Position:** Reformed Baptist (Calvinistic)
- **Focus:** Rediscovering and implementing early Christian church practices as described in the New Testament

**Key Characteristics:**
- Participatory worship (1 Cor 14:26 - "each one has" pattern)
- Lord's Supper as full meal (weekly)
- Small/house-based churches
- Dialogical teaching
- Elder-led congregational consensus

**Meeting Structure: Three-Phase Meeting**

**Phase 1: Lord's Supper (Full Meal)**
- Weekly observance
- Full meal (not just bread and cup)
- Communal participation
- Focus on remembering Christ's death

**Phase 2: Participatory Worship**
- "Each one has" pattern (1 Cor 14:26)
- Believers in good standing can use spiritual gifts
- Songs, testimonies, Scripture readings, exhortations, praise
- Purpose: build up the gathered saints

**Phase 3: Teaching**
- Dialogical teaching (discussion-oriented)
- Not just one-way preaching
- Interactive learning

**Key Quotes:**

**From NTRF Website:**
> "First-century church meetings were characterized by 'each one has' (1Co 14:26). Accordingly, believers in good standing with the church are free to use their spiritual gifts to build up the gathered saints through songs, short testimonies, Scripture readings, exhortations, or praise."

**On Participatory Worship:**
> "How did Jesus and the Apostles design church meetings so as to motivate people to greater depths of discipleship? In Jesus' original design, there was considerable spontaneity and freedom for congregants to address the church each week. Church meetings were not a spectator sport!"

**On the Principle:**
> "The prime directive was that anything said had to be crafted to edify (strengthen, build up, encourage) the congregation. Everything combined to motivate believers to greater obedience to the commands of the Lord, a key aspect of discipleship."

**On Order:**
> "Let all things be done for building up" (1 Cor 14:26). Everything must be for edification. Only one person should speak at a time. Restraint was required for the greater good.

**Significance:** One of the most explicit modern advocates for the "each one has" participatory worship pattern, with a clear three-phase meeting structure that separates Lord's Supper from participatory worship.

### 2. Early Baptists and Baptist History

**Background:**
- **Particular Baptists (Reformed/Calvinistic):** Emerged in England in 1630s-1640s
- **First London Baptist Confession (1644/1646):** Reformed Baptist confession that NTRF aligns with
- **Theology:** Calvinistic (doctrines of grace), congregational polity
- **Connection to NTRF:** NTRF is theologically Baptist (Reformed/Calvinistic) but practically restorationist

**Reference:** See `Baptist-History-Writings/` folder for detailed analysis

**Early Baptist Practices (17th Century):**

**What Early Baptists Had:**
- ✅ **Congregational polity** - Local church autonomy, congregational decision-making
- ✅ **Regenerate membership** - Emphasis on regenerate church membership, church discipline
- ✅ **House-based meetings** - Met in homes (necessity due to persecution)
- ✅ **Memorial view of Lord's Supper** - Not transubstantiation
- ❓ **Weekly communion** - Unclear from historical records, likely monthly/quarterly
- ❓ **Participatory worship** - Unclear, likely pastor-led (emphasis on preaching suggests monologue)

**Later Baptist Practice (18th-20th Centuries):**

**What Became Standard:**
- **Monthly/quarterly communion** - Most common (rarely weekly)
- **Pastor-led services** - Structured, monologue sermons
- **NOT participatory** - No "each one has" pattern
- **Building-based meetings** - Not house-based
- **Congregational singing** - Yes, but not participatory sharing

**NTRF's Distinctiveness:**

**What Makes NTRF Different from Traditional Baptists:**

**1. Weekly Communion:**
- **NOT traditional** for Reformed Baptists (typically monthly)
- **NTRF advocates weekly** - Restoration of early church practice
- **Calvin wanted weekly** - But wasn't widely adopted by Baptists

### 4. John Calvin (Reformed Tradition)

**Background:**
- **John Calvin (1509-1564)** - Reformer and theologian
- **Author of "Institutes of the Christian Religion"** - Final edition 1559
- **Leader of Reformation in Geneva** - Founder of Reformed/Presbyterian tradition
- **1537 Articles to Geneva Council** - Expressed desire for weekly communion

**Reference:** See `Reformed-History-Writings/` folder for detailed analysis

**Weekly Communion:**

**Calvin's Position:**
- **Weekly communion** - Advocated for weekly observance (at least)
- **"At least once a week"** - Minimum frequency
- **"Very frequently"** - Preferred even more frequent observance
- **Every Sunday** - Desired for Holy Supper to be celebrated every Sunday when congregation assembles

**Quote from Institutes (IV, 17, 44, and 46):**
> "The Lord's Supper should be administered at least once a week"

**From 1537 Articles to Geneva Council:**
> "The Holy Supper should be celebrated at least every Sunday when the congregation assembles"

**Why It Wasn't Adopted:**
- **Geneva Town Council / City Magistrates opposed** - Primary opposition
  - Had authority over church practices (Calvin did NOT have absolute rule)
  - Concerned about logistics, misunderstandings, potential superstition
  - Limited communion to quarterly (4 times per year: Christmas, Easter, Pentecost, first Sunday in September)
- **The "Libertines" opposed** - Faction of wealthy, politically powerful families
  - Led by Ami Perrin (prominent syndic)
  - Opposed Calvin's reforms and authority
  - Wanted to maintain traditional practices
- **Calvin's response** - Documented his disagreement in public records, hoping future generations would correct it
- **Most Reformed churches** - Adopted monthly/quarterly communion (following Geneva's practice)
- **Result:** Gap between Calvin's desire and actual practice - Calvin wanted weekly but had to compromise with quarterly

**Theology:**
- **Spiritual presence** - Believers partake through Holy Spirit and elevation of soul to heaven
- **Means of grace** - Divine gift to sustain and strengthen faith
- **Unity** - Fosters unity among Christians
- **Remembrance** - Frequently call to mind sufferings of Christ

**Participatory Worship (Limited):**

**What Calvin Advocated:**
- **Active congregational participation** - Not passive experience
- **Congregational singing** - All sing psalms together (Geneva Psalter)
- **Responsive prayers** - Congregation responds
- **Communal Lord's Supper** - All believers partake together
- **Mutual edification** - Promotes understanding and engagement

**What Calvin Did NOT Advocate:**
- **NOT "each one has" pattern** - Did not advocate for 1 Corinthians 14:26 pattern
- **NOT open participation** - Not open participation from all members
- **NOT spontaneous** - Structured liturgy, not spontaneous
- **NOT lay teaching** - Preaching was minister-led

**Quote from Institutes:**
> "The congregation should be actively involved in the service."

> "The Lord's Supper is not merely a ritual performed by clergy but a communal act where all believers partake together, reflecting the unity and mutual edification of the church body."

**Significance:** Calvin advocated for weekly communion and active congregational participation, but his weekly communion desire was not widely adopted in Reformed churches. His participatory worship was limited to congregational singing and responsive prayers, not the "each one has" pattern. This shows a gap between ideal and practice - a major Reformer wanted weekly communion but it wasn't adopted.

## PART 7: LARGE CHURCHES AND SMALL GROUPS

### The Principle: Participatory Worship Only Viable in Small Groups

**The User's Insight:**
> "In a large church the participatory model is only viable in small groups"

**Reference:** See `Large-Churches-Small-Groups-Participatory-Worship.md` for detailed analysis

### The Problem with Large Churches

**Challenges:**
- **Time constraints** - In a 200-person meeting, if everyone gets 5 minutes, that's 1,000 minutes (16+ hours)
- **Coordination** - Who speaks when? How to prevent chaos?
- **Intimacy loss** - Harder to know everyone, less personal connection
- **Practical logistics** - Sound system, space, time management

**Historical Pattern:**
> "As gatherings moved to larger basilicas, participatory worship was replaced with more structured performances by clergy, leading to a loss of the practical reality of the priesthood of all believers." (NTRF)

### The Solution: Small Groups Within Large Churches

**The Pattern:**
- **Large main service** - Structured, clergy-led liturgy (weekly communion)
- **Small groups** - Participatory worship, mutual edification
- **Both together** - Complete church experience

- Fully participatory worship
- Weekly communion (when priest available)
- Scripture-centered
- Within larger parish structure
