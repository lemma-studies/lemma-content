## Driving Question

**Did Jesus drink alcoholic wine, and what does Scripture teach about wine as a substance — distinct from the sin of drunkenness?**

### Sub-questions

1. What did the biblical vocabulary distinguish — Hebrew *yayin* / *tirosh* / *shekar*, Greek *oinos* / *gleukos* / *sikera*?
2. Is "new wine" (νέος οἶνος, Matt 9:17; γλεῦκος, Acts 2:13) unfermented grape juice or fermented wine at a certain stage?
3. Does Scripture distinguish moral verdicts on wine-as-substance from wine-consumption-to-drunkenness?
4. Where does the 19th-century American "two-wine theory" come from, and how does it hold up as exegesis?

## Scoping decisions

1. **Substance vs. abuse distinction** is the load-bearing analytical move. The study distinguishes wine-as-substance (permitted, given, blessed) from consumption-to-drunkenness (universally condemned).
2. **Neutral stance on the two-wine theory.** Present its argument at its strongest, follow the evidence, let the reader see the reasoning. No editorial verdict beyond what the evidence supports.
3. **Denominational latitude.** Communion-substance practice (fermented vs. grape juice) is a denomination-defining question. The study presents the historical shape (1st-c wine → 19th-c American temperance → modern denominational split) without advocacy for a particular practice.
4. **Verification discipline.** Every quotation goes through `npm run verify:study` before the manuscript is called done. Modern sources (Nott 1857, Patton 1874, Bacchiocchi 1989) are archive.org-verifiable; ancient sources are ANF/NPNF/CCEL-verifiable.
