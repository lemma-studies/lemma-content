## Why the Early Church, Roman Sources, and Archaeology Matter

The text of Revelation identifies Pergamum as the location of "Satan's throne" and the place where Antipas was martyred, but it does not explain *what* the throne was or *how* Antipas died. For this, we must look to the historical and archaeological record: Roman writers who described Pergamum, early Christian writers who commented on the Revelation text and preserved traditions about Antipas, and the physical remains of the city itself.

**Important caution:** Absence of a teaching in early church writings may mean it wasn't apostolic teaching OR simply wasn't their urgent concern. Arguments from silence require humility. Similarly, later traditions about Antipas may reflect genuine historical memory or may be legendary embellishment. We must evaluate each source on its own terms.

## Part II: The Archaeological Evidence

### The Great Altar of Zeus (The "Pergamon Altar")

**Discovery:** Excavated by Carl Humann beginning in 1878 for the Royal Museums of Berlin. The altar is now reconstructed in the Pergamon Museum, Berlin.

**Description:** A massive U-shaped structure approximately 36 meters wide and 34 meters deep, with a monumental staircase and a frieze depicting the Gigantomachy (battle of the gods against the giants). The altar sat on the acropolis of Pergamum, visible from the entire city and from the road approaching the city.

**Why it matters:**
- The altar's shape --- a massive throne-like platform with a grand staircase --- is visually the most "throne-like" structure in the ancient city
- Built during the reign of Eumenes II (197-159 BC)
- Dedicated primarily to Zeus Soter ("Zeus the Savior") and Athena Nikephoros ("Athena the Victory-Bearer")
- The Gigantomachy frieze depicted the triumph of the Olympian gods over primordial forces --- a visual theology of divine authority and power

### The Temple of Augustus and Roma (Imperial Cult Temple)

**Discovery and dating:** Built 29 BC by decree of Augustus, who permitted the provincial assembly (*koinon*) of Asia to build a temple to himself and the goddess Roma at Pergamum.

**Description:** [To be completed --- archaeological remains, location on the acropolis, relationship to other buildings]

**Why it matters:**
- This was the first temple to a living Roman emperor in the province of Asia
- It established Pergamum as the *neokoros* (temple warden) of the imperial cult
- The provincial imperial cult required annual ceremonies, oaths, and sacrifices --- participation was essentially a test of loyalty to Rome
- Refusal to participate was not merely religious dissent but political treason --- punishable by death under the *ius gladii* of the proconsul

### The Asklepion

**Description:** A massive healing complex located outside the city walls, connected to the acropolis by a colonnaded sacred way. Featured a treatment center, incubation halls (where patients slept to receive healing dreams from Asklepios), a theater, a library, and a sacred spring.

**The serpent connection:**
- Asklepios was represented by and identified with the serpent
- Living serpents were kept in the Asklepion and allowed to crawl over patients
- The serpent symbol of Asklepios (a snake wrapped around a staff) remains the symbol of medicine today
- For early Christians, who identified Satan as "that old serpent" (Revelation 12:9, 20:2), the Asklepion's serpent worship would have been unmistakably satanic

## Part III: Early Christian Sources on Pergamum and Antipas

### The Martyrdom of Antipas (Traditions)

**Note on sources:** Scripture tells us only that Antipas was "slain among you" in Pergamum. Later traditions provide additional details about the manner of his death, but these traditions must be evaluated carefully. The earliest detailed account is the *Acts of Antipas* (also called *Martyrdom of Antipas* or *Vita Antipae*), a hagiographic text of uncertain date (possibly 5th-6th century, though claiming to describe 1st-century events).

#### The *Acts of Antipas* (*Martyrium Sancti Antipae*)

**Context:** A hagiographic account, likely composed in the 5th-6th century but claiming to preserve earlier tradition. Preserved in Greek manuscripts.

> [Full quotation to be researched and inserted --- the key passage describing Antipas's martyrdom]

**Source:** [To be identified --- Acta Sanctorum, Bollandist edition, or other critical edition]

**The tradition:** According to the *Acts*, Antipas was the bishop of Pergamum who was arrested during the reign of Domitian (81-96 AD) for refusing to worship the emperor and the pagan gods. He was placed inside a bronze bull (or brazen bull) --- an execution device shaped like a bull, heated from beneath --- on the grounds of the Temple of Artemis (or, in some versions, near the Asklepion). He prayed throughout his ordeal and died praising God.

**Evaluation:**
- The brazen bull tradition echoes the legend of Phalaris of Akragas (Sicily), who is said to have used such a device in the 6th century BC. This literary parallel raises questions about whether the detail is historical or borrowed.
- The association with Domitian's reign aligns with the traditional dating of Revelation to c. 95 AD.
- The refusal to worship the emperor is historically plausible given Pergamum's status as the imperial cult center.
- The *location* of the execution (at a specific temple) varies between manuscripts, making it difficult to identify the exact site.

## Part IV: Roman Administrative Context

### Pergamum as Provincial Capital

**Key facts to document:**
- Pergamum was the capital of the Roman province of Asia from its creation in 133 BC (when Attalus III bequeathed the Pergamene kingdom to Rome)
- The Roman proconsul of Asia --- one of the most powerful governors in the empire --- had his official seat (*sedes*) in Pergamum (later shared with Ephesus and Smyrna in a rotation)
- The proconsul held the *ius gladii*, the right of the sword --- the power to execute criminals and, critically, those convicted of treason (*maiestas*), which included refusal to worship the emperor
- The connection between the proconsul's *sedes* (seat) and Satan's *thronos* (throne) may be deliberate in the Revelation text
