## Word 1: throne --- *thronos* (θρόνος, Strong's G2362)

**Greek:** θρόνος
**Root meaning:** A stately seat, a throne, a chair of state having a footstool
**Semantic range:** Throne, seat of authority, seat of honor. In classical Greek, used for the seats of gods, kings, and judges. In the LXX, translates the Hebrew *kisse'* (כִּסֵּא).

**Usage in Revelation:**
- Revelation 1:4 --- the seven spirits "before his throne" (God's throne)
- Revelation 2:13 --- "where Satan's seat [throne] is" (Satan's throne in Pergamum)
- Revelation 4:2-6 --- God's throne in heaven, described in detail
- Revelation 13:2 --- "the dragon gave him his power, and his seat [throne], and great authority" (Satan gives his throne to the beast)
- Revelation 16:10 --- "the fifth angel poured out his vial upon the seat [throne] of the beast"
- Revelation 20:4 --- "I saw thrones, and they sat upon them, and judgment was given unto them"
- Revelation 20:11 --- "a great white throne"
- Total uses in Revelation: 47 (out of 62 in the entire NT)

**Translation comparison:**

| Translation | Revelation 2:13 Rendering | Notes |
|-------------|--------------------------|-------|
| KJV | "Satan's seat" | Uses "seat" rather than "throne" |
| ESV | "Satan's throne" | More literal |
| NASB | "Satan's throne" | More literal |
| LSB | "the throne of Satan" | Most literal, preserving article |
| NIV | "where Satan has his throne" | Paraphrase |
| YLT | "the throne of the Adversary" | Translates *Satanas* as title |

**Significance for the study:** *Thronos* in Revelation is never used loosely. It always denotes an actual seat of established, ruling authority. In 2:13, it places Satan's presence in Pergamum in the same category as God's throne in heaven (4:2) and the beast's throne (13:2, 16:10). This is not metaphorical or atmospheric --- it is a claim about enthroned power.

## Word 2: dwelleth --- *katoikei* (κατοικεῖ, Strong's G2730)

**Greek:** κατοικέω (katoikeō)
**Root meaning:** To dwell, settle down, inhabit. Compound of *kata* (down, intensifying) + *oikeō* (to dwell).
**Semantic range:** To settle permanently, to take up residence, to make one's home. Distinguished from *paroikeō* (to dwell as a stranger/sojourner --- Strong's G3939) and *menoō* (to remain/abide temporarily --- Strong's G3306).

**Usage in key passages:**
- Revelation 2:13 --- "where Satan *dwelleth*" --- Satan is a permanent resident of Pergamum
- Matthew 2:23 --- "he came and *dwelt* in a city called Nazareth"
- Acts 7:4 --- "he removed him into this land, *wherein ye now dwell*"
- Colossians 1:19 --- "in him should all fulness *dwell*" (permanent residence of divine fullness in Christ)
- Colossians 2:9 --- "in him *dwelleth* all the fulness of the Godhead bodily"

**Significance for the study:** The verb *katoikei* means Satan has *settled* in Pergamum. This is not a temporary visit, a passing influence, or a periodic manifestation. Satan has made Pergamum his permanent home. The intensified form (*kata-oikeo*) emphasizes the thoroughness of this residence.

## Word 3: martyr/witness --- *martus* (μάρτυς, Strong's G3144)

**Greek:** μάρτυς (martys, martus)
**Root meaning:** A witness, one who testifies to what they have seen, heard, or know.
**Semantic range:** In classical Greek and early NT usage, simply "witness" (legal or otherwise). The development toward "one who dies for testimony" began in this period and accelerated after passages like Revelation 2:13. The English word "martyr" derives directly from this Greek word.

**Usage in key passages:**
- Revelation 1:5 --- Jesus is "the faithful witness" (*ho martus ho pistos*)
- Revelation 2:13 --- Antipas is "my faithful martyr/witness" (*ho martus mou ho pistos mou*)
- Revelation 3:14 --- Jesus is "the faithful and true witness" (*ho martus ho pistos kai alethinos*)
- Revelation 11:3 --- the two witnesses (*tois dusin martusin mou*)
- Revelation 17:6 --- "the blood of the martyrs/witnesses of Jesus"
- Acts 1:8 --- "ye shall be witnesses (*martures*) unto me"

**Significance for the study:** Antipas receives the same title as Jesus Himself: *ho martus ho pistos* (the faithful witness). This is an extraordinary honor. In the semantic development from "witness" to "martyr," Revelation 2:13 is a pivotal text --- Antipas witnesses by dying.

## Word 4: sharp sword --- *rhomphaia* (ῥομφαία, Strong's G4501)

**Greek:** ῥομφαία (rhomphaia)
**Root meaning:** A large, broad sword; a Thracian broadsword.
**Semantic range:** In classical Greek, a large barbarian sword. In the NT, used only in Luke 2:35 (figuratively, a sword piercing Mary's soul) and six times in Revelation (1:16, 2:12, 2:16, 6:8, 19:15, 19:21), always associated with Christ's judicial authority.

**Distinguished from:** *Machaira* (μάχαιρα, Strong's G3162) --- a short sword, dagger, or knife. *Machaira* is used for the Roman sword carried by soldiers and governors (Romans 13:4, "he beareth not the sword in vain"). The *rhomphaia* is something greater.

**Significance for the study:** Jesus does not carry a *machaira* (the Roman official sword) but a *rhomphaia* (a great sword). This may be a deliberate contrast: the Roman proconsul at Pergamum had the *machaira* (the *ius gladii*), but Jesus has the *rhomphaia* --- the greater sword, the ultimate judicial authority.

## Word 5: Antipas --- *Antipas* (Ἀντιπᾶς)

**Greek:** Ἀντιπᾶς (Antipas)
**Root meaning:** Likely a contracted form of *Antipatros* (Ἀντίπατρος), meaning "against the father" or "in place of the father" or "like the father."

**Usage:**
- Revelation 2:13 --- the only occurrence in the entire Bible
- The name *Antipatros* was common in the Hellenistic world (e.g., Antipater of Macedon, Herod Antipas)

**Significance for the study:** This is the only named martyr in the Book of Revelation. Despite the importance of martyrdom throughout the book (6:9-11, 7:14, 11:3-12, 17:6, 20:4), only Antipas is named. The etymology "against the father" could be meaningful if the "father" in question is Zeus (called "father of gods and men") --- Antipas stood against the pagan father-deity. However, this is speculative.

## Word 6: Pergamos --- *Pergamos* (Πέργαμος)

**Greek:** Πέργαμος (Pergamos) or Πέργαμον (Pergamon)
**Root meaning:** Possibly from *pergos/pyrgos* (πύργος) meaning "citadel" or "tower," referring to the elevated acropolis.

**Historical forms:**
- *Pergamos* --- the form used in the Textus Receptus (and thus KJV)
- *Pergamon* --- the more common classical Greek form
- *Pergamum* --- the Latinized form used by Roman writers

**Significance for the study:** The name itself means "citadel" --- appropriate for a city whose acropolis was one of the most dramatically elevated in the ancient world, rising approximately 300 meters above the surrounding plain. The very name evokes height, power, and dominion.

## Word 7: two edges --- *distomos* (δίστομος, Strong's G1366)

**Greek:** δίστομος (distomos)
**Root meaning:** Two-mouthed, two-edged. Compound of *dis* (twice) + *stoma* (mouth).
**Semantic range:** Having two edges (of a sword). Used in Hebrews 4:12 and Revelation 1:16, 2:12.

**Cross-reference:**
- Hebrews 4:12 --- "sharper than any twoedged sword" --- the Word of God
- Revelation 1:16 --- "out of his mouth went a sharp twoedged sword" --- Christ's appearance

**Significance for the study:** The two-edged sword is the word/judgment of Christ. In the context of Pergamum, where the Roman *ius gladii* determined life and death, Jesus claims the ultimate judicial authority.
