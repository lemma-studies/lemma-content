# Conclusion — Love and Unity in Christ

## The Shared Ground

However a reader adopts and applies the faithful views, all Christian
views on the parent-and-adult-child question presuppose a common
ground. Naming it explicitly, before the final practical
exhortations, is not a matter of picking a view. It is a matter
of naming what every faithful reading of Scripture on this
question presupposes.

- **Christ is the treasure.** "Yea doubtless, and I count all things
  but loss for the excellency of the knowledge of Christ Jesus my
  Lord: for whom I have suffered the loss of all things, and do
  count them but dung, that I may win Christ" (Phil. 3:8). No view
  presented in §5.3 disagrees. The household arrangement, however
  ordered, is subordinate to the treasure.
- **The Kingdom is the field of hidden treasure.** "Again, the
  kingdom of heaven is like unto treasure hid in a field; the
  which when a man hath found, he hideth, and for joy thereof
  goeth and selleth all that he hath, and buyeth that field"
  (Matt. 13:44). Every arrangement in §5.3 is legitimate only as
  it releases the household — parent, adult child, and the whole
  fabric of household life — for the kingdom's sake.
- **The believer's life is hid with Christ in God.** "If ye then
  be risen with Christ, seek those things which are above, where
  Christ sitteth on the right hand of God. Set your affection on
  things above, not on things on the earth. For ye are dead, and
  your life is hid with Christ in God. When Christ, who is our
  life, shall appear, then shall ye also appear with him in glory"
  (Col. 3:1-4). The parent-and-adult-child question is important
  and it is not ultimate. What is ultimate is Christ.

Every Christian view in §5.3 is a view of *how* to live these
convictions in the household. No view in §5.3 is a substitute for
them. Where a view begins to displace these convictions — where
the household arrangement, of whatever style, becomes the treasure
instead of the vessel of the treasure — it has ceased to be
Christian, whatever its structural resemblance to a defensible
view.

The convictions above are the *shared ground.* The views are
different fields plowed on the same ground.

## Love and Unity

The New Testament pattern is clear at its poles. Minor children under
the parental roof obey and honor. Married adults form new households
and cleave to their spouses. The middle case — the unmarried adult
son or daughter — is left, deliberately, to be worked out by faithful
households under the Lordship of Christ, in the community of the
church, according to the honest reading of the texts we have.

The Bible does not give one household template. It gives:

- One command that never expires: **honor thy father and thy mother**.
- One paradigmatic transition: **leaving unto cleaving** in marriage.
- One first allegiance: **Christ above every earthly bond**.
- One inviolable boundary: **no tradition may nullify honor**.
- One direction of provision: **parents for children, then children
  for parents** as the years turn.

Within those, the Bible permits a range of faithful arrangements. It
does not permit any arrangement that fails the love-and-unity test.

This study has surveyed the faithful views (1a, 1b, 2a, 2b, and the
orthogonal Arrangement Question). It ends not with a further survey
but with a summons to four sets of readers — and to the church
that shepherds them all.

**To the unmarried daughter reading this.** Your ultimate question
is not *which household template your family follows,* and it is
not *whether you will marry.* Your ultimate question is whether the
God who calls Himself the husband of His people (Isa. 54:5; 2 Cor.
11:2; Rev. 19:7-9) is enough. He is. You are not incomplete for
lack of a husband; you are already betrothed. Whether the LORD
gives you an earthly marriage or not, you are the beloved of the
Bridegroom now. Set your affections on Him first, and every other
question — including whether to marry, whom to marry, and how to
honor your parents through the process — falls into its place.

**To the unmarried son reading this.** Your task is not to *wait*
for a wife; your task is to *become* the man Christ is calling you
to be. Elisabeth Elliot's counsel to her generation of daughters
runs to sons with equal force. Manhood in Scripture is *given
under call* — Elisha at the plow, Peter at the nets, Timothy from
Lois and Eunice's knee to Ephesus — and the call is Christ's before
it is your father's, your mother's, or your future wife's. Honor
your parents; take their counsel seriously; and where Christ is
sending, go — do not let waiting itself become the calling.
Do the next thing He has given you, today.

**To the parent releasing an adult child.** Your love outlasts your
control. You have formed this child as well as you have been given
grace to form. Now the task is not further formation by decree; it
is intercession without restraint, and dedication to the LORD.
Hannah lent Samuel to the LORD "as long as he liveth" — not for a
season. Mary released her Son to a cross her release did not
prevent. Neither woman controlled the outcome; both are honored.
Your adult son or daughter belongs first to Christ. Release him
there, and keep releasing him there, in prayer, for as long as you
draw breath.

**To the parent whose adult child has walked away.** The father in
Luke 15 does not run when the son turns around; he runs when the
son is "yet a great way off." He was watching the whole time. That
watching — Monica's seventeen years of tears for Augustine, Samuel's
mourning until the LORD said *how long?*, the widow of Nain's silent
grief that Christ met before she could ask — is not a lesser
faithfulness. It is the parental posture of release joined to
intercession-without-restraint (§5.0.1-§5.0.2). Keep praying. Keep the door open. Do not make
honor conditional on your child's return; do not chase; do not
relent on your household's confession. Do the next thing the LORD
has given you today, and trust the ending to Him.

**To the church that shepherds all of these:** equip them. Do not
require of any family a household template Scripture does not
require; do not permit a template Scripture forbids (§1.8). Marry
your young who are called to marriage. Care for and deploy your
unmarried. Honor your elders. Bring the marriages of your young
under pastoral counsel and blessing — the assumption Ignatius
could take for granted (§3.4). And
teach the reader to walk his own case through Scripture,
Christocentrism, Antiquity, and Reality before the Lord (Chapter 6).

*"And he shall turn the heart of the fathers to the children, and
the heart of the children to their fathers."* — Malachi 4:6.

*Soli Deo Gloria.*
